## Key Question 1: The core academic papers — text used, prompt, horizon, alpha, critiques

### Takeaway
The 2023–2024 "first wave" (Lopez-Lira & Tang; Chen–Kelly–Xiu; Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev; Jha et al.) established that GPT-4-class models extract return- and fundamentals-relevant signal from news, financial statements, and transcripts, with effects concentrated in small caps and short horizons; the 2024–2026 "second wave" is mostly about whether those backtests survive look-ahead bias, with new tooling (anonymization, chronologically-trained models, lookahead-propensity tests, point-in-time benchmarks) and generally smaller but still positive out-of-sample effects.

### Cited Findings

**Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023, updated through 2025), "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements? Return Predictability and Large Language Models" (WP; SSRN 4412788 / arXiv 2304.07619; a chapter version appears in a Wiley volume "The Predictive Edge" and an IEEE version exists)** [snippet]
- Text: >50,000 news headlines about NYSE/Nasdaq/small-cap-exchange stocks from a data vendor, starting October 2022 (after ChatGPT's then training cutoff) — [CNBC summary](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/chatgpt-may-be-able-to-predict-stock-movements-finance-professor-says.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4412788).
- Task/prompt: ChatGPT asked whether a headline is good, bad, or irrelevant ("unknown") news for the firm's stock price; answers mapped to a numeric score (+1/−1/0) and correlated with next-day returns — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/chatgpt-may-be-able-to-predict-stock-movements-finance-professor-says.html). (Exact prompt wording could not be verified from the PDF in this session; widely reproduced as a "pretend you are a financial expert… answer YES/NO/UNKNOWN then explain in one sentence" instruction — see Gaps.)
- Horizon: daily (next-day) returns; also "initial reaction" vs "subsequent drift" in later versions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619).
- Results (later versions): using post-cutoff headlines, GPT-4 "captures initial market responses, achieving approximately 90% portfolio-day hit rates for the non-tradable initial reaction," and GPT-4 scores "significantly predict the subsequent drift, especially for small stocks and negative news" — [arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619).
- Model comparison: GPT-1, GPT-2, and BERT "cannot accurately forecast returns," so return predictability is framed as an emergent capability of larger models; ChatGPT outperforms traditional sentiment methods — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619); [Larry Swedroe summary](https://larryswedroe.substack.com/p/can-chatgpt-forecast-stock-price).

**Chen, Kelly & Xiu (Nov 2022; revised), "Expected Returns and Large Language Models" (WP; SSRN 4416687; won 2023 GSU-RFS FinTech Conference Best Paper)** [snippet]
- Text: firm-level financial news across 16 global equity markets in 13 languages; LLM embeddings (ChatGPT/OpenAI embeddings and the LLaMA family) mapped to next-period returns — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416687); [Wharton Jacobs Levy slides](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Kelly-WhartonJL.pdf).
- Results: LLM-news signals carry information beyond reversal and firm characteristics; "substantially outperform" bag-of-words and Word2Vec; long-short portfolios on LLM-implied expected returns "deliver economically meaningful Sharpe ratios after transaction costs and are not driven by look-ahead bias"; short-horizon "news momentum" consistent with underreaction — persists several days for small stocks, dissipates quickly for large stocks; analyst-related news topics most profitable — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416687).
- (Specific Sharpe figures were not retrievable in this session; see Gaps.)

**Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev (May 2024), "Financial Statement Analysis with Large Language Models" (WP; BFI WP 2024-65; SSRN; arXiv 2407.17866)** [full text — BFI PDF]
- Text: no narrative at all — only anonymized, standardized balance sheets and income statements reconstructed from Compustat (1968–2021 fiscal years; 150,678 firm-years, 15,401 firms), formatted per Capital IQ's balancing model, with firm names and dates removed and relative years (t, t−1) used — [BFI WP 2024-65 PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Model/settings: `gpt-4-0125-preview`, temperature 0, top-p 1, no max_tokens cap, `logprobs` enabled to get token-level log-probabilities — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Prompts (paraphrase of the paper's own description; the appendix shows the statement format, not verbatim prompt text): (a) a "simple" prompt presenting the two statements and asking whether earnings will increase or decrease next period; (b) a Chain-of-Thought prompt that instructs the model to take the role of a financial analyst, (i) identify notable changes in line items, (ii) compute key ratios without restricting the ratio set, stating formulas before computing, (iii) give economic interpretations of the ratios, then predict direction, write a rationale paragraph, and output a magnitude category (large/moderate/small) and a confidence score in [0,1] — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Accuracy: analysts (consensus, 1 month after filing) 52.71% accuracy / 54.48% F1; GPT simple prompt 52.33% / 54.52%; GPT CoT 60.31–60.35% accuracy, F1 63.45%; a narrowly trained ANN on the same numeric data 60.45% / 61.62%; logistic regression (Ou & Penman style) 52.94% / 57.23%. Analysts improve to 55.95% and 56.58% at 3 and 6 months — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Model family comparison (20% random subsample): GPT-4 61.05%, Gemini 1.5 Pro 59.15%, GPT-3.5-turbo only 52.29% (F1 59.17%); GPT-4 and Gemini disagree on only ~6% of firm-years (1,808 of 30,135) — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Confidence calibration: top-quartile confidence (self-reported) 62.44% vs ~2.6 pp lower for bottom quartile; using average token log-prob as confidence, 63.15% vs ~4.6 pp lower; predicted "large" changes 62.03% vs "small" 60.22% — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Trading test: portfolios formed June 30 each year, held one year; GPT long/short constructed from (direction = increase/decrease) ∧ (magnitude ∈ {moderate, large}) then ranked by average token log-prob to keep 10% of firms on each side. Equal-weighted long-short Sharpe 3.36 (ANN 2.54, logit 2.05); value-weighted GPT 1.47 vs ANN 1.79; after 10 bp transaction cost the EW Sharpe is 2.84 and VW 0.95. Monthly alpha after FF5+momentum: EW 84 bp (≈10%/yr); VW 37 bp (t = 2.43); FF3 alpha "exceeds 12% per year" — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Look-ahead tests: (1) asked GPT to guess firm name from the anonymized statements — 0.07% correct (below random), with the model almost always naming Tesla/Facebook/Amazon; year guesses 2.95% correct and almost always 2019–2021 regardless of true year; same under the CoT prompt (500-obs sample). (2) Out-of-training-window test: FY2022 statements → FY2023 earnings (released early 2024, after GPT-4-Turbo's April 2023 cutoff): accuracy 58.96%, F1 63.91%, vs ANN 59.10% / 61.13% — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Mechanism: BERT embeddings of GPT's CoT narratives fed to an ANN reach 58.95% accuracy / 65.26% F1 and correlate 94% with GPT's own forecasts; narrative + numeric dual-input model reaches 63.16% / 66.33% — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Caveats stated by the authors: accuracy trends down ~0.1 pp/yr over 54 years (5.4 pp total) with sharp drops in 1974, 2008–09, 2020; GPT accuracy is only 54.36% for FY2021 — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf).
- Secondary coverage: [SUERF policy brief](https://www.suerf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SUERF-Policy-Brief-1008_Kim-et-al.pdf); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/05/223-gpt-4-performs-better-than-humans-at-financial-statement-analysis-says-study/).

**Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev (2023), "Bloated Disclosures: Can ChatGPT Help Investors Process Information?" (WP; arXiv 2306.10224; Chicago Booth)** [snippet]
- Text: MD&A sections of 10-Ks and earnings-call transcripts; GPT-3.5-Turbo summaries. Unconstrained summaries are "dramatically shorter, often by more than 70%," yet explain market reactions better than the originals; a "Bloat" measure (length of original relative to summary) is associated with lower price efficiency and higher information asymmetry — [arXiv 2306.10224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10224); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/chatgpt-could-help-investors-make-more-informed-decisions).

**Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev (Oct 2023), "From Transcripts to Insights: Uncovering Corporate Risks Using Generative AI" (WP; arXiv 2310.17721; BFI WP 2023-132)** [snippet]
- Text: earnings-call transcripts Jan 2018–Mar 2023; GPT-3.5-Turbo generates risk summaries and risk assessments for political, climate, and AI-related risk; GPT-based measures "possess significant information content and outperform existing risk measures in predicting firm-level volatility and firms' choices such as investment and innovation" (benchmarked against Hassan et al. 2019 bigram-based measures) — [arXiv 2310.17721](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17721); [BFI PDF](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/BFI_WP_2023-132.pdf); Hassan et al. (2019, QJE, PR) baseline — [QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/134/4/2135/5531768).

**Jha, Qian, Weber & Yang (2023/2024), "ChatGPT and Corporate Policies" (WP; NBER w32161; SSRN 4521096; Chicago Booth 23-15)** [snippet]
- Text: earnings conference-call transcripts; ChatGPT produces a firm-level "investment score" of managers' anticipated capex changes; validated against CFO survey responses and interpretable text — [NBER w32161](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32161/w32161.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4521096).
- Effect sizes: +1 SD investment score → +0.034 SD capex next quarter; −1.80% annualized raw return, −1.47% FF5-adjusted, −1.40% q5-adjusted in the following quarter; abnormal returns persist up to 9 quarters; extended to dividend and employment scores — [NBER w32161](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32161/w32161.pdf); [NY Fed slides](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2023/FinTech/3_400pm_Yang_SLIDESChatGPT_and_Corporate_Policies.pdf).

**Eisfeldt, Schubert, Zhang (& Taska) (2023), "Generative AI and Firm Values" (WP; NBER w31222; SSRN 4436627) and Eisfeldt & Schubert (2024) "AI and Finance" (NBER w33076)** [snippet]
- Not a filing-text paper per se: exposure is built from each firm's occupational mix (task-level GenAI exposure), not 10-K text. "Artificial-Minus-Human" portfolio (long high-exposure, short low-exposure) earned 0.44%/day in the two weeks after ChatGPT's release and >9% cumulative through 31 Mar 2023; +10 pp exposure → +7.6 pp probability of mentioning GenAI on earnings calls — [NBER w31222](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31222/w31222.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4436627); [VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/generative-ai-and-firm-valuation); data at [artificialminushuman.com](https://artificialminushuman.com/).

**2025–2026 follow-ups and replications** [snippet]
- Anic, Barbon, Seiz & Zarattini (Oct 2025), "ChatGPT in Systematic Investing – Enhancing Risk-Adjusted Returns with LLMs" (WP; arXiv 2510.26228; SSRN 5680782): S&P 500 constituents + high-frequency news; prompt tells ChatGPT a stock is about to enter a momentum portfolio and asks whether recent news supports continuation; LLM-enhanced long-only momentum raised annualized return 15%→18%, Sharpe 0.57→0.69, max drawdown −33%→−31%; robust to transaction costs, prompt variants, and constraints; strongest for concentrated portfolios; includes a true out-of-sample period after the model's pretraining cutoff — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26228); [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/chatgpt-in-systematic-investing-enhancing-risk-adjusted-returns-with-llms/); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/chatgpt-momentum-investing/).

- "Decoding risk sentiment in 10-K filings: Predictability for U.S. stock indices" (2025, Finance Research Letters, PR): five tone indicators from 10-K Item 1A using Loughran–McDonald plus GPT-3.5-turbo-0125, GPT-4, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini calibrations; aggregated risk-factor tone predicts one-week-ahead S&P 500 returns; GPT-4-based tone had the best predictive accuracy — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612325007317).

### Inferences
- The strongest *filing*-specific (as opposed to news) result with a trading test is Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev 2024, and it uses *numbers* from the statements, not narrative text — the narrative-text return results are either index-level (risk-factor tone → S&P 500), or embedded in embeddings-plus-ML pipelines where attribution to "LLM reading" is weaker.
- Effects are consistently concentrated in small caps, negative news, and short horizons (days for news, quarters for transcript-derived policy scores, one year for statement-based earnings direction) — exactly where capacity and transaction costs bind.
- GPT-3.5-class models underperform GPT-4-class models by ~8 pp accuracy on the same task (52.3% vs 60.3% in KMN), so results from 2023 papers using GPT-3.5 likely understate current-model capability but also predate the strongest look-ahead concerns about newer models trained on more recent text.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve verbatim prompt text for Lopez-Lira & Tang, Jha et al., or "From Transcripts to Insights" (PDF hosts blocked). The KMN 2024 paper describes but does not print its prompt in the body; Appendix B shows the statement format only.
- Chen–Kelly–Xiu Sharpe ratios by market, and Jha et al. chunking/temperature details, not retrievable.
- "From Text to Alpha" (arXiv 2510.03195) and "Supply Chain Propagation" (arXiv 2606.29290) content unavailable beyond titles.
- No peer-reviewed replication of Lopez-Lira & Tang with post-2024 models located.

## Key Question 2: LLMs on 10-K risk factors, MD&A, 8-K, Form 4, 13D; LLM-derived exposure measures (AI-washing, cyber, political/climate, labor, geopolitical)

### Takeaway
Most LLM-derived exposure measures to date come from earnings calls (following Hassan et al.) or 10-K Item 1A/Item 1; AI-washing and cyber-risk measures built with LLMs show short-lived announcement effects and cross-sectional premia, while I found no LLM-based academic study of 8-K, Form 4, or Schedule 13D text with return results.

### Cited Findings
- **AI-washing (10-K).** Li (Univ. of Florida, 2025, WP), "AI Washing": 10-Ks 2018–2023; a fine-tuned BERT distinguishes dimensions of AI disclosure and LLMs filter AI discussion on whether the firm is actively investing, outsourcing, or forward-looking; firms with AI overclaiming see a short-lived positive reaction (BHAR ≈2.29% at filing, fading to 1.81% by day +1 and insignificant beyond +2) and, in another specification, a 1.6% CAR / 1.4% BHAR decline after 10-K release; AI disclosure predicts performance only when backed by AI-related labor investment — [UT Dallas-hosted PDF (Aug 2025)](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.utdallas.edu/dist/8/1090/files/2025/09/AI_Washing_Boyuan_Li.pdf); [July 2025 version](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.utdallas.edu/dist/8/1090/files/2025/08/AI-Washing.pdf) [snippet].
- Campbell et al., "Artificially Intelligent or Artificially Inflated? Determinants…" (Utah Winter Accounting Conference 2025, WP) — [PDF](https://www.utah-wac.org/2025/Papers/campbell_UWAC.pdf); "How Accurate Are Corporate AI Disclosures?" (CLS Blue Sky, Mar 2025) — [Columbia](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2025/03/03/how-accurate-are-corporate-ai-disclosures/); "AI washing: Strategic disclosure and backlash" (Finance Research Letters 2026, PR) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612326002151) [snippet].
- **AI risk / cyber risk from Item 1A with three LLMs.** A Stockholm School of Economics-hosted paper ("Artificial Intelligence Risk") builds a firm-level AI-risk exposure index from 10-K Item 1A for S&P 500 firms with scores generated independently by ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini (multi-dimensional, including cybersecurity); the index predicts the cross-section of returns and CARs around cyber events — [SSE PDF](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=6805) [snippet].
- **Cyber risk (pre-LLM NLP baselines).** Florackis et al. (2023, RFS, PR): 10-K-derived cyber-risk score; high-score portfolios earn up to 8.3%/yr premium. Celeny & Maréchal (2023/2024, WP; arXiv 2402.04775): doc2vec trained on MITRE ATT&CK applied to full 10-Ks; sorted portfolios up to 18.72%/yr; follow-up "Disentangling the sources of cyber risk premia" (arXiv 2409.08728) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04775); [WEIS PDF](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.utdallas.edu/dist/e/1380/files/2024/03/Celeny-Marechal-WEIS-2024-2b398e501aecec2b.pdf) [snippet].
- **Political/climate/AI risk (earnings calls).** Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev "From Transcripts to Insights" (above): GPT-3.5 risk summaries/assessments outperform Hassan-style bigram measures in predicting volatility and investment — [arXiv 2310.17721](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17721) [snippet].
- **Geopolitical risk.** Federal Reserve FEDS Note (Aug 2025), "Measuring Geopolitical Risk Exposure Across Industries: A Firm-Centered Approach" — [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/measuring-geopolitical-risk-exposure-across-industries-a-firm-centered-approach-20250829.html); Boston Fed (2025) on firms' perceived geopolitical risk and investment — [Boston Fed](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/current-policy-perspectives/2025/how-firms-perceptions-of-geopolitical-risk-affect-investment.aspx) [snippet; method details not retrieved].
- **Labor / human capital.** "Disclosed Human-Capital Disruption and Firm-Specific Risk" (2026, WP; arXiv 2608.14859) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14859) [title only].
- **Risk-factor extraction tooling.** "Taxonomy-Aligned Risk Extraction from 10-K Filings with [LLMs]" (Jan 2026, arXiv 2601.15247) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15247); "Utilizing Pre-trained and Large Language Models for 10-K Items Segmentation" (Feb 2025, arXiv 2502.08875) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08875); "A Scalable Data-Driven Framework for Systematic Analysis of SEC 10-K Filings Using LLMs" (Sep 2024, arXiv 2409.17581) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.17581) [titles/snippets].
- **Negative/null result on risk-factor tone changes.** A public replication (41 firms, FY2014–2023, 376 filing-years) found year-over-year 10-K risk-factor tone (FinBERT vs Loughran–McDonald) does not predict forward returns and FinBERT does not beat the dictionary — [GitHub ZacharyChai/finbert-10k-sentiment](https://github.com/ZacharyChai/finbert-10k-sentiment) (non-peer-reviewed, tiny sample).
- **Form 4.** "Insider filings as trading signals — Does it pay to be fast?" (Finance Research Letters 2024/2025, PR) uses intraday data around Form 4 filings to test prompt-reaction strategies (no LLM) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612324015435). A QuantInsti tutorial covers Form 4 filing-date event studies in Python — [QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/sec-form-4-insider-trading-python-event-study/) [snippet].
- Form 4 is structured XML (sampled 2025 filings were ~90–190 words of text after tag-stripping), so there is little narrative for an LLM to read beyond footnotes — [own computation on sec.gov Archives, 2025-Q4 index].

### Inferences
- The economically useful LLM applications on 10-K text so far are *classification/measurement* (what kind of AI/cyber/political exposure, is the disclosure substantive) rather than direct return forecasting; returns come from sorting on those measures.
- 8-K/Form 4/13D are mostly structured or short; the LLM value-add there is extraction and normalization (e.g., parsing Item 5.02 departures, 13D "purpose of transaction" text) rather than sentiment. I found no academic evidence either way.

### Gaps
- No academic paper located that runs an LLM over 8-K text or Schedule 13D text for returns.
- Could not verify the SSE "AI risk" paper's prompt, scale, or inter-model agreement numbers.
- Supply-chain exposure via LLM (Hassan-style) — only the embeddings-based 2026 arXiv paper surfaced; no details.

## Key Question 3: Prompt-engineering findings (zero-shot vs CoT, structured output, scales, YoY comparison, chunking, ensembling, temperature, extraction vs classification, inter-run consistency)

### Takeaway
The best-documented finding is that a structured chain-of-thought "act as an analyst, compute ratios, interpret, then decide" prompt lifts GPT-4 from coin-flip to 60% on earnings direction, that self-reported confidence and token log-probs are informative for ranking, and that run-to-run variability in open-ended stock-picking prompts is large (42–79% of picks change) unless outputs are aggregated across repeated runs.

### Cited Findings
- **Zero-shot vs CoT:** simple prompt 52.33% vs CoT 60.31% accuracy on earnings direction (GPT-4-Turbo, temperature 0); CoT also produces narratives whose embeddings alone reach 59% — [KMN 2024, BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text].
- **Output schema that worked (KMN 2024):** direction (increase/decrease) + magnitude (large/moderate/small) + confidence ∈ [0,1] + rationale paragraph; portfolio construction used direction ∧ magnitude filter, then ranked by average output-token log-probability — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text].
- **Three-way label scale (Lopez-Lira & Tang):** good/bad/unknown (YES/NO/UNKNOWN) mapped to +1/−1/0 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/chatgpt-may-be-able-to-predict-stock-movements-finance-professor-says.html) [snippet].
- **Temperature / determinism:** KMN set temperature 0, top-p 1 "to ensure minimal variability" and enabled logprobs — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text].
- **Inter-run consistency:** Chon, Kim & Kim (2025, Finance Research Letters, PR; SSRN 5350936), "Multifaceted variability in LLM-driven stock recommendations": repetition variability (same query repeated) changes 42–79% of selected stocks on average and is highly sensitive to the system prompt; rephrase variability changes only 6–11%; recommended remedy is to keep stocks recommended with high probability across many identical/rephrased runs; LLM portfolios "struggle to consistently outperform the market on a risk-adjusted basis" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612325021762); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5350936) [snippet].
- **Cross-model agreement as an ensemble check:** GPT-4 vs Gemini 1.5 Pro disagree on ~6% of earnings-direction calls — [KMN 2024](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text]; the SSE AI-risk paper scores Item 1A independently with ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini — [SSE PDF](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=6805) [snippet].
- **Context/"conditioning" prompts:** Anic et al. inform the model that the stock is entering a momentum portfolio and ask whether news supports continuation; the score conditions both selection and weights; results robust to prompt design — [arXiv 2510.26228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26228) [snippet].
- **Hierarchical chunk-summarize for long documents:** ECC Analyzer (2024, WP; arXiv 2404.18470) splits earnings-call documents into chunks, summarizes each with an LLM, then summarizes the summaries; reports a 27.7% reduction in prediction error vs prior methods — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18470) [snippet].
- **Function-calling / structured JSON extraction of risk factors:** a practitioner write-up describes giving Claude 4.5 Sonnet the full Item 1A text with a task-specific system prompt and structured-output function calling to return a list of risks with free-form tags and supporting quotes, then diffing against last year's filing — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/llm-financial-document-analysis) (vendor/blog, V) [snippet].
- **Anonymization to remove "distraction":** Glasserman & Lin replace company names with fictional placeholders (e.g., "Google" → "Blahblahblah"); in-sample, anonymized headlines *outperformed* non-anonymized, which they attribute to a "distraction effect" (general knowledge of the firm interfering with sentiment measurement) that is larger than look-ahead bias, especially for large firms — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Assessing-Look-Ahead-Bias-in-Stock-Return-Generated-Glasserman-Lin/af42ae531ad0c41518fcf3a382e6e9f1ba465601); [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17322); published in Journal of Financial Data Science 6(1):25 (PR) — [PM Research](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijjfds/6/1/25) [snippet]. Related: "Anonymization and Information Loss" (2025, arXiv 2511.15364) and "Same Company, Same Signal: The Role of Identity in Earnings Call Transcripts" (2024, arXiv 2412.18029) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15364); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18029) [titles].
- **Extraction accuracy of local/open models:** Kansas City Fed RWP 23-12 "Evaluating Local Language Models" applies open models to a financial-text task (details not retrieved) — [KC Fed](https://www.kansascityfed.org/documents/9862/rwp23-12cookkazinnikhansenmcadam.pdf) [title].
- **Model-choice sensitivity:** GPT-3.5 ≈ chance on KMN's task while GPT-4/Gemini 1.5 ≈ 60% — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text].

### Inferences
- A production prompt for a fund should (i) fix temperature 0 and still run ≥3 samples (or 2–3 models) and aggregate, since single-run variability is documented even at the level of stock selection; (ii) force a schema with direction/score, magnitude, confidence, and quoted evidence; (iii) strip firm identifiers and absolute dates when the goal is a backtest rather than a live read; (iv) use the CoT "compute, interpret, decide" pattern for numeric sections and a separate extraction pass for narrative.
- Log-probabilities as a confidence proxy only exist on APIs that expose them (OpenAI); for Claude/Gemini one must rely on self-reported confidence or sampling agreement.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found comparing scoring scales (−1..1 vs 1..5 vs 1..10) on filings; no study found on "compare this year's section to last year's" prompts with return results (the "From Text to Alpha" paper may do this but was unreadable).
- No published inter-run consistency numbers for filing-level scoring at temperature 0 (the 42–79% figure is for open-ended stock recommendations).

## Key Question 4: Evaluation — look-ahead bias, point-in-time timestamps, acceptance datetime, survivorship, transaction costs

### Takeaway
Look-ahead bias is now the central methodological battleground: the literature offers four defenses — test only after the model's training cutoff, anonymize entities/dates, use chronologically trained models (ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT, DatedGPT, "point-in-time" LMs), and diagnose contamination directly (Lookahead Propensity, Look-Ahead-Bench, HindsightBench) — while EDGAR's acceptance-datetime vs filing-date rules determine what was tradable when.

### Cited Findings
- **Sarkar & Vafa (2024/2025), "Lookahead Bias in Pretrained Language Models" (WP; ICML 2025 Workshop on Reliable and Responsible Foundation Models):** LLMs trained on unrestricted internet corpora "inevitably embed information from the future," compromising backtests and causal inference — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Lookahead-Bias-in-Pretrained-Language-Models-Sarkar-Vafa/e5097ccd4477c454e0df52945cef4ba74d255b91); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379767258_Lookahead_Bias_in_Pretrained_Language_Models) [snippet]. KMN cite it as motivation for their post-cutoff test — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text].
- **Glasserman & Lin (2023; JFDS 2024, PR):** distinguishes look-ahead bias (model knows subsequent returns) from distraction (firm knowledge interferes); out-of-sample look-ahead is moot but distraction remains, so anonymization is useful live as well as in backtests — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17322) [snippet].
- **Gao, Jiang & Yan (Dec 2025, rev. Jun 2026), "Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts" (WP; arXiv 2512.23847; SSRN 5985277):** a "date-only recall query" (firm name, ticker, target date, nothing else) estimates Lookahead Propensity (LAP) — the probability the model has internalized the realized outcome; LAP is materially positive in-sample and "collapses essentially to zero right after the training-data cutoff"; applied to headlines→returns and transcripts→capex tasks; companion "Debiasing LLMs by Fine-tuning" (arXiv 2604.02921) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5985277); [arXiv 2604.02921](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02921v2) [snippet].
- **Chronologically consistent models:** He, Lv, Manela, Wu (2025), "Chronologically Consistent Large Language Models" (arXiv 2502.21206) train ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT on date-restricted corpora; follow-ups "Instruction Tuning Chronologically Consistent Language Models" (arXiv 2510.11677), "Scaling Point-in-Time Language Models" (2026, arXiv 2607.11889), "DatedGPT: Preventing Lookahead Bias… with Time-Aware Pretraining" (2026, arXiv 2603.11838), "Pretraining Language Models on Historical Text" (2026, arXiv 2606.02991). Point-in-time models "eliminate this leakage by construction, but existing efforts typically produce models that lag substantially behind their unconstrained counterparts" — [arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206); [arXiv 2510.11677](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11677); [arXiv 2607.11889](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11889v2); [arXiv 2603.11838](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11838v2) [snippets/titles].
- **Benchmarks:** "Look-Ahead-Bench: a Standardized Benchmark of Look-ahead Bias in Point-in-Time LLMs for Finance" (Jan 2026, arXiv 2601.13770) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13770); "HindsightBench: A Black-Box Behavioral Audit Protocol for Parametric Hindsight in Time-Indexed LLM Decision Tasks" (2026, arXiv 2607.18867) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18867); "Summoning the Oracle to Slay It: Mitigating Look-Ahead Bias in Financial Backtesting with LLMs" (2026, arXiv 2605.24564) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564); "ChatGPT as a Time Capsule: The Limits of Price Discovery" (2026, arXiv 2604.21433) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21433v1) [titles only].
- **Post-cutoff evaluation in practice:** Lopez-Lira & Tang sample starts Oct 2022 after ChatGPT's cutoff — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/chatgpt-may-be-able-to-predict-stock-movements-finance-professor-says.html); KMN evaluate FY2022→FY2023 outside GPT-4-Turbo's April 2023 window — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf); Anic et al. report a "truly out-of-sample period after the model's pre-training cut-off" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26228).
- **EDGAR filing-date vs acceptance rules (SEC):** submissions starting transmission at or before 5:30 p.m. ET get that day's filing date; after 5:30 p.m. they receive the next business day's filing date (6:00 a.m. ET) and "will not be disseminated by EDGAR until the next business day." Exception: 20 submission types — 3, 3/A, 4, 4/A, 5, 5/A, 144, 144/A, and the *MEF/POS 462B registration types — transmitted 5:30–10:00 p.m. ET keep that day's filing date and are disseminated until 10:00 p.m. ET — [SEC "Determine the status of my filing"](https://www.sec.gov/submit-filings/filer-support-resources/how-do-i-guides/determine-status-my-filing) [SEC, full page]; Regulation S-T Rule 13 background — [Latham & Watkins](https://wow.lw.com/Article/Index/186) [snippet].
- **Practical consequence:** a 10-K accepted at 6 p.m. on Jan 27 carries acceptance datetime Jan 27 but filing date Jan 28; backtests keyed on filing date will mis-time the information release — [search snippet referencing EDGAR hours](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/filing/edgar/hours_deadlines.php); Hudson Labs blog "Filing after hours: when it matters" — [Hudson Labs](https://www.hudson-labs.com/post/filing-after-hours-when-it-matters) (V, blocked).
- **Dissemination latency:** filings "typically appear on sec.gov within 1-3 minutes of the EDGAR system timestamp," with no guarantee under load; the submissions API has "a typical processing delay of less than a second" and XBRL APIs "under a minute"; nightly bulk ZIPs republished ~3:00 a.m. ET; daily indexes update starting ~10:00 p.m. ET — [SEC Developer FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/os/webmaster-faq); [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces); [Accessing EDGAR Data](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) [SEC].
- **Survivorship / point-in-time universe:** the EDGAR full-index files (1994Q3–present) list every filing with CIK, form type, filing date, and path, giving a survivorship-free filing universe; the `submissions.zip` bulk file carries `acceptanceDateTime` per filing — [Accessing EDGAR Data](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data); [EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) [SEC].
- **Transaction costs in the papers:** KMN's EW Sharpe falls from 3.36 to 2.84 and VW from 1.47 to 0.95 with 10 bp one-way costs — [BFI PDF](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/financial-statement-analysis-w/BFI_WP_2024-65.pdf) [full text]; Chen–Kelly–Xiu and Anic et al. report results net of transaction costs (figures not retrieved) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416687); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26228) [snippet].

### Inferences
- A defensible evaluation protocol for a fund: (1) record EDGAR `acceptanceDateTime` and only let the signal trade at the next tradable print after dissemination (next open for post-5:30 p.m. filings, except Section 16 forms); (2) anonymize issuer and absolute dates in prompts for the backtest window; (3) run Gao–Jiang–Yan's date-only recall probe on the backtest universe to measure LAP by year and discard/haircut years with LAP > 0; (4) report results separately for the post-training-cutoff window of whichever model is used; (5) use the full-index as the universe to avoid survivorship.
- Because the strongest effects are in small caps, a 10 bp cost assumption is optimistic; KMN's VW results (Sharpe 0.95 net) are the more realistic ceiling for a capacity-constrained strategy.

### Gaps
- Could not read Look-Ahead-Bench, ChronoGPT, or Sarkar–Vafa results tables (magnitudes of bias by task).
- No source retrieved that quantifies what fraction of 10-K/10-Q/8-K filings are accepted after 5:30 p.m. ET (Hudson Labs' post likely has this).

## Key Question 6: Costs, throughput, filing volumes, EDGAR access rules, full-text search, XBRL/Financial Statement datasets

### Takeaway
Running a frontier model over every 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, Form 4 and Schedule 13D/13G filed in a year is roughly a 1.5–3.5 billion-input-token job (≈450k documents), i.e., on the order of $10k–$40k/yr at list prices for a Sonnet/Opus-class model before batch discounts — cheap relative to data-vendor fees; the binding constraints are EDGAR's 10 req/s limit, the after-hours dissemination rules, and parsing quality, not model cost.

- 10-K: 6,690 (plus 10-K/A 713; NT 10-K 732). 10-Q: 17,289 (10-Q/A 289). 8-K: 67,651 (8-K/A 1,468).

- Financial Statement Data Sets: numeric face financials from XBRL (SUB/NUM/TAG/PRE tables, "segments" field added Dec 2024), quarterly, Jan 2009–Jun 2026, 3.4–122 MB per quarter; SEC disclaims accuracy — [SEC FSDS](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/financial-statement-data-sets).
- Financial Statement *and Notes* Data Sets: text and detailed numeric data from statements and notes (adds TXT tables), monthly since Nov 2020 (one year of monthly files retained, older consolidated to quarterly), Jan 2009–Aug 2026, monthly files 41–314 MB, quarterly 250–800+ MB — [SEC FSNDS](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/financial-statement-notes-data-sets).
- Developer-community notes on 403/429 handling and section parsing — [DealCharts](https://dealcharts.org/blog/sec-edgar-api-guide); [dev.to practical guide](https://dev.to/odeeb/the-sec-edgar-api-a-practical-guide-to-free-filing-data-in-python-15b) [snippets]. Vendors offering real-time filing streams and 10-K item-extraction APIs: sec-api.io, EdgarTools (open-source Python), EODHD — [sec-api.io](https://sec-api.io/); [EdgarTools](https://edgartools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/concepts/sec-filings/); [EODHD](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis-blog/sec-filings-api) [snippets].
- Section parsing research: LLM/PLM-based 10-K item segmentation (arXiv 2502.08875) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08875); every 10-K MD&A extraction write-up — [Medium](https://medium.com/@jgfriedman99/every-10-k-mda-in-edgar-0de0de5282b5) [snippets].

### Inferences
- At ~450k relevant filings/yr and 10 req/s, raw fetching is trivial (<1 day even at 1 req/s with one fetch per filing); the engineering effort is in (a) polling for new accessions at sub-minute cadence during 6 a.m.–10 p.m. ET, (b) correctly stamping `acceptanceDateTime` vs filing date, (c) stripping inline-XBRL wrappers and exhibits, and (d) item segmentation.
- Form 4 dominates filing count (351k) but is <10% of tokens; 10-Qs dominate tokens. A tiered pipeline (cheap model for triage/extraction on 8-K/Form 4/13G; frontier model with CoT on 10-K/10-Q sections and 13D narrative) keeps the frontier-model bill in the low tens of thousands of dollars.
