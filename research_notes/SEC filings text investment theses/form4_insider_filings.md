## KQ1. Baseline evidence: do insider trades predict returns, and what survives once "routine" trades are removed?

### Takeaway
Across four decades of US data, open-market insider *purchases* predict positive abnormal returns (roughly 0.4–0.8%/month in classic samples, concentrated in small caps) while *sales* on average do not; Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski (2012) show that essentially all of the purchase alpha comes from "opportunistic" insiders (no calendar-month trading routine), whose value-weighted long-short portfolio earns 82 bp/month, and Ali and Hirshleifer (2017) show four-factor alphas above 1%/month when opportunism is identified from pre-earnings-announcement trade profitability.

### Cited Findings
- Lakonishok & Lee (2001), *Review of Financial Studies* 14(1):79–111, NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq 1975–1995: insiders are contrarian in aggregate and predict market movements better than simple contrarian strategies; cross-sectional predictive power is driven by small firms; informativeness comes from purchases, insider selling has no predictive ability — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/ouprfinst/v_3a14_3ay_3a2001_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a79-111.htm); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5216856_Are_Insider_Trades_Informative)
- Aggregator figure attributed to Lakonishok & Lee: small-cap stocks bought by insiders earned ~7.4% abnormal return over the following 12 months (secondary source, not verified against the paper) — [QuantDecoded](https://quantdecoded.com/en/insider-trading-signals-informative-trades)
- Jeng, Metrick & Zeckhauser (2003), *Review of Economics and Statistics* 85(2):453–471, US insider transactions 1975–1996, performance-evaluation (calendar-time portfolio) approach: insider purchases earn abnormal returns of more than 6% per year; insider sales earn no significant abnormal returns; expected cost of insider trading to non-insiders ≈ 10 cents per $10,000 transaction — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v85y2003i2p453-471.html); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=146029)
- Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (2012), "Decoding Inside Information," *Journal of Finance* 67(3):1009–1043: trades are classified as "routine" or "opportunistic" based on when in the year the trade occurs and whether the insider has a history of similar past trades; a portfolio strategy focusing solely on opportunistic traders yields value-weighted abnormal returns of 82 bp/month, while routine traders generate essentially zero abnormal returns; opportunistic trades predict future firm-specific news, analyst-forecast, management-forecast and earnings-announcement returns; opportunistic traders are more likely to face SEC enforcement and cut trading after enforcement waves; the most informed opportunistic traders are local, non-executive insiders at geographically concentrated, poorly governed firms — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01740.x); [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/apr11/decoding-inside-information); [HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=40609); [NBER WP 16454](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16454/w16454.pdf)
- Aggregator restatement: the opportunistic long-short portfolio's 0.82%/month ≈ 9.8% annualized in the historical sample; broad insider-purchase portfolios earn ~37–47 bp/month — [IBKR Campus summary](https://ibkrcampus.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/stocks/what-corporate-insider-buying-can-tell-investors-evidence-from-academic-research/)
- Ali & Hirshleifer (2017), "Opportunism as a firm and managerial trait: Predicting insider trading profits and misconduct," *Journal of Financial Economics* 126(3):490–515: identify opportunistic insiders by the profitability of their trades before quarterly earnings announcements; strategies based on these insiders' subsequent trades generate four-factor alphas over 1% monthly; such firms show more earnings management, restatements, SEC enforcement, litigation and higher executive pay — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X17302349); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:126:y:2017:i:3:p:490-515); [UCI PDF](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/c/362/files/2020/07/Opportunism.pdf)
- Rozeff & Zaman (1998) and Lakonishok & Lee (2001) document that insiders typically buy after price declines and sell after run-ups; Piotroski & Roulstone (2005, *JAE*) find insider trades reflect both contrarian beliefs and superior knowledge about future cash-flow realizations — [ScienceDirect (Piotroski & Roulstone)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410104000382); [Hull repository summary](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/436869)
- Cziraki & Gider (2019 WP, "The Dollar Profits to Insider Trading") exists as a dollar-profit complement to the percentage-return literature — [Wharton PDF](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/CZiraki.pdf)

### Inferences
- The routine/opportunistic split is the single most important filter: the published CMP rule (an insider who traded in the same calendar month in each of the prior three years is "routine"; others are "opportunistic") is directly implementable from Form 4 histories because Form 4 gives insider identity (CIK), transaction date and code. Note: the three-consecutive-years/same-month definition is from the paper as generally described; full text could not be fetched here to re-verify the exact wording.
- The Ali & Hirshleifer approach (score each insider by past pre-earnings-announcement trade profitability) is a second, orthogonal "who" filter that can be computed from Form 4 plus earnings dates.
- Both filters are built from structured fields, not footnotes; footnotes add value mainly by removing non-discretionary trades (see KQ4) before the routine/opportunistic classification is run.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve t-statistics, equal-weighted results, or purchase-vs-sale split for CMP's 82 bp/month; the NBER/HBS/SSRN PDFs were blocked.
- Seyhun (1986, *JFE*) and Seyhun (1998, *Investment Intelligence from Insider Trading*) effect sizes were not retrievable (search budget exhausted before that query ran).

## KQ2. Cluster buying, purchase/sale asymmetry, insider role, buying after declines, small caps, indirect accounts

### Takeaway
Purchases by multiple insiders within days of each other roughly double the one-month abnormal return relative to solitary purchases (≈2.1% vs ≈1.2%; 3.8% vs 2.0% over 21 trading days in a vendor backtest); CFO purchases beat CEO purchases by ~5 percentage points over 12 months; trades booked through family/trust/retirement accounts ("indirect" ownership on Form 4) outperform direct-account trades by ~50 bp in the following month; and the signal is strongest in small/micro caps.

### Cited Findings
- Alldredge & Blank (2019), "Do Insiders Cluster Trades with Colleagues? Evidence from Daily Insider Trading," *Journal of Financial Research*: ~23% of insider purchases occur on the same day as another insider's purchase at the same firm; purchases within two days of another insider's purchase are followed by ~2.1% abnormal return in the next month, ~0.9 pp more than solitary purchases; clusters occur in periods of high information asymmetry (measured by subsequent earnings surprises) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfir.12172); [IBKR Campus summary](https://ibkrcampus.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/stocks/what-corporate-insider-buying-can-tell-investors-evidence-from-academic-research/)
- 2iQ Research (vendor backtest, sample not specified in snippet): over 21 trading days, cluster purchases earn ~3.8% abnormal vs ~2.0% for non-cluster purchases; the gap widens to ~2.5% over 90 days; "high conviction" purchases earned 20.94% 12-month excess return vs 1.32% (medium) and −3.40% (low) in one study they review — [2iQ: Guide to Cluster Buying](https://www.2iqresearch.com/blog/what-is-cluster-buying-and-why-is-it-such-a-powerful-insider-signal); [2iQ: Profiting from Insider Transactions](https://www.2iqresearch.com/blog/profiting-from-insider-transactions-a-review-of-the-academic-research)
- Practitioner definition used by Verity/InsiderScore-style scoring: a cluster buy = two or more distinct insiders at the same company transacting in the same direction within 7 days — [InsiderBrief methodology page](https://insiderbrief.io/articles/how-insiderscore-works) (note: this page describes InsiderBrief's own "InsiderScore," which may differ from Verity's product of the same name)
- Cluster signals are more frequent and more impactful in small- and mid-caps where insider ownership is proportionally larger and analyst coverage thinner; excess returns from cluster buys are front-loaded in the first 21–60 trading days and decay thereafter; more recent work shows reduced but still measurable excess returns — [Form4API cluster-buy guide](https://www.form4api.com/guides/cluster-buy-signals) (practitioner, no sample stated)
- Wang, Shin & Francis (2012), "Are CFOs' Trades More Informative Than CEOs' Trades?", *JFQA* 47(4):743–762, 1992–2002: CFOs earn an average 12-month excess return 5% higher than CEOs after purchases; superior performance survives risk controls and persists after public disclosure; CFO purchases are associated with more positive future earnings surprises — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/are-cfos-trades-more-informative-than-ceos-trades/B7DD71285F1326E694CC860193C87710); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Are-CFOs%E2%80%99-Trades-More-Informative-Than-CEOs%E2%80%99-Trades-Wang-Shin/bda53b91a75f5fdd131dfde19b73648d01f696c1)
- "Indirect Insider Trading" (*JFQA*, 2022/2023; KU-affiliated authors): compares direct personal-account trades with indirect trades via family members, trusts, retirement accounts and foundations; a hedge portfolio of large indirect purchases and sales earns 0.90% abnormal return in the following month, 50 bp more than direct trades; hedge portfolio on all indirect family trades accumulates 6.34% after 2 years; outperformance persists up to 2 years and does not reverse; indirect trades better predict earnings surprises and large price changes — [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7933F3093D5055F73128AF294FFF0945/S0022109022001119a.pdf/indirect-insider-trading.pdf); [KU News (Dec 2022)](https://news.ku.edu/2022/12/08/indirect-insider-trades-prove-more-lucrative-informed-ones-study-finds)
- Hvide & Nielsen, "Flying below the radar: Insider trading by executives below the top," *JFE* (2026 issue; SSRN 3957199): below-top executives earn ~68–101 bp abnormal return over one month and ~250 bp over six months on own-company purchases; top executives earn smaller, often insignificant abnormal returns; same individuals do not outperform in unrelated or same-industry stocks. **Caveat: Norwegian register data 1997–2014, not US Form 4** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X2600053X); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3957199); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/insider-trading-by-executives-below-the-top/)
- CMP (2012): the most informed opportunistic traders are local, non-executive insiders at geographically concentrated, poorly governed firms (i.e., not necessarily the CEO) — [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/apr11/decoding-inside-information)
- Insider purchases in financially distressed firms and after corporate downsizing are the subject of 2025–2026 *JCF*/*Pacific-Basin* papers ("The veracity of insider trading signals in financially distressed firms"; "Tell me you have a plan! Insider trade signals from firms undergoing corporate downsizing") — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927539826000289); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0929119926000647) (abstracts not retrievable; effect sizes unknown)

### Inferences
- A practical "consensus buy" thesis: ≥2 distinct Section 16 insiders with code P on Table I within 7 calendar days, in firms below ~$2B market cap, excluding routine traders; expected one-month abnormal return in the 2–4% range historically, with most of the effect inside 60 trading days.
- Role matters but not monotonically: CFO > CEO in US data; non-executive/local directors are the top opportunistic group in CMP; below-top executives are informed in Norwegian data. Form 4's "Relationship of Reporting Person" block (Director / Officer (give title) / 10% Owner / Other) plus the officer title text are the fields to parse.
- The "indirect" (I) ownership flag plus the "Nature of Indirect Beneficial Ownership" column (and its footnote, e.g., "by spouse," "by family trust," "by 401(k)") is a structured-plus-text feature with a documented ~50 bp/month edge over direct trades.

### Gaps
- No US-specific effect size was found for "insider purchases after large price declines" as a stand-alone conditional strategy (only the general contrarian finding).
- Purchase-vs-sale asymmetry in *recent* (post-2010) US samples is covered only indirectly (see KQ9).

## KQ3. Rule 10b5-1 plans: pre-reform abnormal returns, the 2022/2023 amendments, and post-reform evidence

### Takeaway
Pre-reform, 10b5-1 plan sales were demonstrably informed (Jagolinzer 2009; Larcker et al. 2021: adoption-to-first-trade gaps under 30 days, single-trade plans, and plans starting just before earnings all foreshadowed losses avoided); the SEC's December 2022 amendments (effective 27 Feb 2023; Form 4/5 checkbox from 1 Apr 2023; Item 408 from fiscal periods beginning on/after 1 Apr 2023) added a 90–120-day cooling-off, a mandatory Form 4 checkbox with plan-adoption date in the footnotes, and quarterly Item 408(a) adoption/termination disclosures. Post-reform evidence conflicts: a *JAE* paper finds plan sales are now followed by flat/slightly positive abnormal returns, while a Bergen/CEPR study of 158,000 sales finds abnormal profits did not fall and migrated into the 90–120-day window after adoption.

### Cited Findings
**Pre-reform evidence**
- Jagolinzer (2009), "SEC Rule 10b5-1 and Insiders' Strategic Trade," *Management Science* 55(2):224–239: >100,000 trades by >3,000 executives in 10b5-1 plans; participants' sales systematically follow positive and precede negative firm performance and generate abnormal forward-looking returns larger than non-participating colleagues; a substantive proportion of randomly drawn plan initiations are associated with pending adverse news; plan adoption is associated with adverse news events occurring an average of 72.2 days after adoption; insiders continue to earn abnormal returns after the fifth planned trade over a 350-day period; evidence that participants terminate sales plans before positive shifts in returns — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1080.0928); [SEC final rule release 33-11138, fns. 19, 110, 112 (p. ~27, 50)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Secondary summary: plan participants' trades outperformed non-participants' by almost 6% — [Mayer Brown deck](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/events/2024/09/10b5-1-presentation.pdf) (unverified against the paper)
- Larcker, Lynch(-Levy), Quinn, Taylor & Tayan (Jan 2021), "Gaming the System: Three 'Red Flags' of Potential 10b5-1 Abuse," Stanford Closer Look Series (note: author list on the published Closer Look is Larcker, Lynch, Quinn, Taylor, Tayan; "Yu" in the assignment does not appear on this piece): data = all restricted-stock sales filed on Form 144 Jan 2016–May 2020 plus the corresponding plan adoption dates; 20,595 plans by 10,123 executives at 2,140 firms; 55,287 sales totaling $105.3B; mean (median) trade $1.9M ($0.4M); mean (median) cooling-off 117.9 (76) days; ~14% of plans commence trading within 30 days and 39% within 60 days of adoption; trades within 30 days of adoption are ~50% larger than trades six or more months later; abnormal returns persist for all plans up to 60 days after adoption, but for single-trade plans (about half of all plans) persist 120 days or more; red flags = (1) short cooling-off, (2) single-trade plans, (3) plans adopted and commencing immediately before earnings; sales under red-flag plans avoid significant losses and foreshadow price declines well in excess of industry peers — [SSRN 3769567](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3769567); [Harvard Corp Gov Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/01/28/gaming-the-system-three-red-flags-of-potential-10b5-1-abuse/); [SEC release 33-11138, fns. 20, 112 and p. ~129](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Mavruk & Seyhun (2016, *Utah Law Review*), "Do SEC's 10b5-1 Safe Harbor Rules Need to Be Rewritten?": use the *voluntary* pre-2023 10b5-1 checkbox/footnote on Form 4, ~14,211 insiders in 3,875 firms per year 2003–2013; insiders trade on information with value for 3–6 months; patterns of selective cancellation — [SEC release 33-11138, p. ~129](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Hugon & Lee (2016) use voluntary 10b5-1 disclosures on Form 4 filed Oct 2000–Dec 2010 — [SEC release 33-11138, p. ~129](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- "When and how are Rule 10b5-1 plans used for insider stock sales?" *JFE* (2023) exists — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X23000697) (contents not retrieved)

**The 2022 amendments (Release 33-11138, "Insider Trading Arrangements and Related Disclosures")**
- Effective date 27 February 2023; Section 16 persons must comply with amended Forms 4 and 5 for reports filed on or after 1 April 2023; Item 408 / 402(x) / 16J disclosures required in the first filing covering the first full fiscal period beginning on or after 1 April 2023 (smaller reporting companies: on or after 1 October 2023) — [SEC release 33-11138, p. 1 and Section III](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Cooling-off for directors and officers: no trades until the later of (1) 90 days after plan adoption/modification or (2) two business days after disclosure of financial results in the 10-Q/10-K for the quarter of adoption, capped at 120 days; 30 days for other persons; none for issuers; a modification that changes amount/price/timing is treated as termination plus new adoption — [SEC release 33-11138](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf); [Mintz summary](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2901/2022-12-27-sec-adopts-amendments-rule-10b5-1-insider-trading)
- Other conditions: director/officer certifications of no MNPI and good faith at adoption; limits on multiple overlapping plans; single-trade plans limited to one per consecutive 12-month period; good-faith operation requirement — [SEC release 33-11138, Section II.A.3](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Forms 4 and 5: mandatory checkbox stating a transaction is pursuant to a plan "intended to satisfy the affirmative defense conditions" of Rule 10b5-1(c); the date of plan adoption must be given in the "Explanation of Responses" (footnote) section; the SEC explicitly expects the checkbox to be combined with Item 408 adoption/termination disclosures to spot opportunistic use; an optional non-10b5-1 checkbox was proposed but not adopted — [SEC release 33-11138, Section II.D](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf); [Form 4 General Instruction 10](https://www.sec.gov/files/form4.pdf)
- Item 408(a) of Regulation S-K: quarterly (10-Q/10-K) disclosure of whether any director or officer adopted or terminated a 10b5-1 plan or non-10b5-1 trading arrangement, with the name and title, adoption/termination date, duration, and aggregate number of securities to be bought or sold (price terms not required); Item 408(b): annual disclosure of insider trading policies; Item 402(x): option grants close in time to MNPI release — [SEC release 33-11138, Section II.B](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf); [Greenberg Traurig summary](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2023/1/sec-adopts-final-amendments-to-rule-10b5-1-and-new-disclosure-requirements)
- Gifts: dispositions by bona fide gift must now be reported on Form 4 within two business days (previously deferrable to Form 5); SEC estimated ~3,000 insiders reported gifts on Form 4 in 2021 vs ~800 on Form 5 (code "G"); the release cites Avci et al. (2021) and Yermack (2009) as motivation and clarifies the 10b5-1 defense is available for gifts — [SEC release 33-11138, Section II.E and p. ~211](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)

**Post-reform evidence (2025–2026)**
- "Insider Trading After the 2022 Rule 10b5-1 Amendment" (Columbia Blue Sky post, 31 July 2025; published in *Journal of Accounting and Economics*, 2026): first large-sample evidence; share of 10b5-1 sales within 90 days of adoption fell from 31.1% before to 1.7% after; after the amendment, plan sales are followed by flat or slightly positive abnormal returns (less successful loss avoidance), insiders are less likely to sell under plans before earnings misses; post-sale abnormal returns also rise for non-plan sales (deterrence spillover); costs: higher compliance burden, modest decline in plan use, and worse price efficiency at more-affected firms — [CLS Blue Sky](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2025/07/31/insider-trading-after-the-2022-rule-10b5-1-amendment/); [ScienceDirect (JAE)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410126000765)
- Contrasting study (University of Bergen; CEPR DP21199 "Regulatory Reform and Opportunistic Insider Trading"; summarized by Verity): 158,000 executive sales 2016–2025 linked to plan adoption dates; reform did not reduce abnormal returns on insider selling and in several specifications they increased after April 2023; trades within 90 days of adoption fell from 35% to under 3% of planned sales, but the 90–120-day window rose from ~11% to nearly 30%, and those trades became more profitable, with annual abnormal gains rising from ~$23M to ~$89M — [Verity summary](https://verityplatform.com/resources/study-insider-sales-10b5-1/); [CEPR DP21199](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21199)
- SEC Investor Advisory Committee (Sept 2021) recommendation and Gensler statement (Dec 2022) frame the policy background — [IAC PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/spotlight/investor-advisory-committee-2012/20210916-10b5-1-recommendation.pdf); [Gensler statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-insider-trading-20221214)

### Inferences
- New text to exploit since April 2023: (1) the Form 4 checkbox (structured XML boolean) plus the adoption date string in the footnote; (2) Item 408(a) paragraphs in 10-Q/10-K giving adoption/termination dates, duration and share counts per named officer; (3) Form 144's "date of plan adoption" field (now electronic, see KQ5). Together these allow computing, for every plan sale, the adoption-to-trade gap, whether the first trade lands in the 90–120-day window, plan size vs holdings, and whether the plan was terminated/modified early.
- Testable theses: (a) sales executed in days 90–120 after adoption, especially first trades and large plan sizes, carry negative information (Bergen result); (b) plan *terminations* disclosed in Item 408(a) without replacement precede positive returns (Jagolinzer's "terminate before positive shifts"); (c) in the JAE result, checkbox-flagged sales can be *removed* from a negative-sales signal to raise its purity.
- The two post-reform studies differ in sample and method; a practitioner should treat the Bergen/Verity numbers (dollar gains, window migration) as the more directly tradeable hypothesis and the JAE result as the null to beat.

### Gaps
- Authors of the JAE 2026 paper and the Bergen/CEPR paper were not captured in the snippets.
- No retrieved study yet measures returns around Item 408(a) termination/modification disclosures specifically (search budget exhausted before this query ran).
- The *JFE* 2023 "When and how are Rule 10b5-1 plans used" findings were not retrieved.

## KQ4. Footnote text in Form 4: what is disclosed and does parsing it add signal?

### Takeaway
Form 4 footnotes ("Explanation of Responses") carry the information that distinguishes discretionary from mechanical trades (tax withholding, option exercise/sale pairs, gifts, trust/family accounts, pledges, 10b5-1 adoption dates); peer-reviewed evidence shows the categories these footnotes identify have very different return content (gifts precede ~4% declines; indirect/family-account trades beat direct trades by ~50 bp/month; contractual-obligation trades have smaller filing-date returns), but no retrieved study has yet shown a *text-mined* footnote feature adding alpha beyond those category labels.

### Cited Findings
- Form 4 instruction 8: if a transaction is not adequately described by a code, "describe the nature of the transaction in the space for explanation of responses"; code J ("Other acquisition or disposition") explicitly requires a description; instruction 10 requires the 10b5-1 plan adoption date in the Explanation of Responses; amendments (Form 4/A) must be explained in footnotes — [SEC Form 4 instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form4.pdf)
- Nearly every variable in the non-derivative and derivative tables has an associated footnote field displayed under "Explanation of Responses"; textual analysis of footnotes opens research avenues prior (Thomson) datasets could not support — [Nature Scientific Data 2023 insider-trading dataset](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02147-6) (full text blocked; from search summary)
- Dissertation (Univ. of Nebraska–Lincoln, AAI3355631) on the economic importance of Form 4 footnotes vs Thomson Reuters data: insider trades caused by contractual obligations (disclosed in footnotes accompanying open-market purchases) earn significantly smaller abnormal returns on Form 4 filing dates — [UNL Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/dissertations/AAI3355631)
- "Are All Insider Stock Sales Created Equal?" (CLS Blue Sky, Jan 2017): examines voluntary footnote disclosures accompanying insider *sales* to test whether they let investors separate discretionary liquidity sales (informative) from non-discretionary liquidity sales — [CLS Blue Sky](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2017/01/05/are-all-insider-stock-sales-created-equal/) (authors/effect sizes not captured)
- Tax withholding (code F) is a mechanical event — shares withheld at vesting/exercise to cover tax; F transactions "generate a large share of what financial media casually labels insider selling" though no discretionary sale occurred; M (exercise) is commonly paired with same-day S or F; signal-construction practice is to filter to P and S only — [Form4API code guide](https://www.form4api.com/guides/form-4-transaction-codes); [13finsight](https://13finsight.com/learn/how-to-read-form-4-transaction-codes-insider-trading); [FinRep reference](https://www.finrep.ai/blog/sec-form-4-transaction-codes-the-definitive-reference)
- Vendor scoring practice (InsiderBrief "InsiderScore"): P receives the highest base score; discretionary S without a 10b5-1 flag a moderate score; option exercises, grants, tax withholding and gifts near-zero; dollar magnitude scaled logarithmically from ~$100K to $10M+; rarity bonus for first-time/infrequent buyers; cluster = 2+ insiders same direction within 7 days — [InsiderBrief](https://insiderbrief.io/articles/how-insiderscore-works)
- Verity/InsiderScore (VerityData) description: cleanses each transaction to strip noise, then applies behavioral flags including cluster buying, buy/sell inflections against a 90-day baseline, cessation of selling, and trades into strength or weakness — [Verity InsiderScore](https://verityplatform.com/solution/veritydata/insiderscore/); [Verity overview PDF](https://verityplatform.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/VerityData_InsiderScore_Overview.pdf); [Verity alpha patterns](https://verityplatform.com/resources/insider-trading-activity-examples/)
- Gifts — Avci, Schipani, Seyhun & Verstein (2021), "Insider Giving," *Duke Law Journal* 71:619–700; essentially all public-company stock transactions since 1986: insider gifts are "suspiciously well-timed" — prices rise ~6% in the year before the gift and fall ~4% in the year after; giving is widespread beyond officers/directors (large shareholders too); substantial evidence of backdating to inflate deductions; access to MNPI matters more than backdating; the SEC adopted the authors' proposal to require Form 4 reporting of gifts within two days — [SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3795537); [Duke LJ](https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/dlj/vol71/iss3/2/); [U-M news](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-business-experts-recommendations-on-insider-giving-incorporated-into-new-sec-rules/); [SEC release 33-11138 fn. 328](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Gifts — Yermack (2009), "Deductio ad absurdum: CEOs donating their own stock to their own family foundations," *JFE* 94:107: price path forms an inverted V over two months centered on the reported gift date, rising then falling by an abnormal ~3% and peaking exactly on the gift date; gifts cluster before negative and after positive earnings announcements; reporting-delay and seasonal evidence consistent with backdating, especially for gifts to the CEO's own family foundation — [NYU PDF](https://web-docs.stern.nyu.edu/glucksman/docs/Gifts_20081231.pdf); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X09001202); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1096257)
- Related: "The gift that keeps on giving: stock returns around CEO stock gifts to family members," *Review of Accounting Studies* (2022) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-022-09732-x) (effect sizes not retrieved)
- Indirect holdings: Form 4 ownership form column (D = direct, I = indirect) plus "Nature of Indirect Beneficial Ownership" and footnotes identify trusts, spouses, children, 401(k)s and foundations; indirect trades outperform direct by 50 bp in month 1 and 6.34% over 2 years for family trades (see KQ2) — [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7933F3093D5055F73128AF294FFF0945/S0022109022001119a.pdf/indirect-insider-trading.pdf)
- Pledged shares: since the August 2006 amendment of Item 403 of Reg. S-K, proxy statements (DEF 14A) must footnote the number of shares pledged by named executives/directors; "Insider pledging: Its information content and forced sale," *Journal of Corporate Finance* (2024): firms with insider pledging show significantly positive one-year abnormal returns after disclosure when the pledgor is a founder, CEO, chairman or inside director; pledges by non-CEO executives and independent directors are not followed by significant abnormal returns — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0929119924001172); [Fabisik, "Why do U.S. CEOs pledge"](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/DKNai479); [NYU Stern (Asija)](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/DO%20INSIDERS%20WHO%20PLEDGE%20THEIR%20SHARES.pdf)
- Filing-violation text: the IFD dataset (arXiv 2507.20162, 2025) compiles >1 million Form 4 transactions 2002–2025 to detect strategic disclosure (late-filing) violations with AI models — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20162)

### Inferences
- Footnote parsing is most valuable as a *denoising* layer: (1) drop S rows whose footnote says "shares withheld to satisfy tax," "sold to cover exercise price," "pursuant to a 10b5-1 plan adopted on [date]" (now also a checkbox), "automatic sale under company's sell-to-cover policy"; (2) re-label J rows using the free text (e.g., gifts, transfers to trusts, distributions from LLCs, forfeitures); (3) tag indirect-account trades by the owner type in the nature-of-ownership text.
- Gifts (code G, now on Form 4 within two days since April 2023) are a *negative* signal on average (−4% over the following year in Avci et al.; −3% over ~1 month in Yermack); large year-end gifts to family foundations by CEOs are the sharpest subset. The timeliness improvement from the 2023 rule makes this newly tradeable at a one-year horizon.
- Pledge text appears in proxies rather than Form 4 (Form 4 footnotes sometimes mention pledges or margin accounts but that is not required; practitioner observation, not sourced); the JCF 2024 result suggests founder/CEO pledges are *not* a bearish signal on average, contrary to intuition — but forced sales after margin calls are a separate event the paper also studies.
- No retrieved paper demonstrates that LLM/NLP features from footnote text, beyond category identification, improve a return forecast. This is a genuine open question suited to a proprietary backtest.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve effect sizes from the UNL dissertation, the CLS Blue Sky 2017 sales-footnote paper, or the RAST 2022 family-gift paper.
- No data found on how frequently each footnote type appears in modern Form 4 XML (Nature 2023 dataset likely has this; blocked).
- No study found on "strategic gift timing" in the post-April-2023 Form 4 regime.

## KQ5. Form 144 notices of proposed sale

### Takeaway
Form 144 is now machine-readable on EDGAR (mandatory electronic filing since 13 April 2023) and discloses broker, proposed share count and value, approximate sale date, sales in the prior three months and — if relying on 10b5-1 — the plan adoption date, with the filer representing no knowledge of MNPI; it was the data backbone of Larcker et al.'s red-flag study, but I found no peer-reviewed paper on the return informativeness of Form 144 filings per se.

### Cited Findings
- Electronic filing of Form 144 on EDGAR became mandatory on 13 April 2023; previously most were filed on paper/fax, viewable in person at the SEC for 90 days and then destroyed; the electronic form is machine-readable and searchable in real time — [SEC compliance-date notice](https://www.sec.gov/oit/announcement/form-144-electronic-filing-compliance-date); [Husch Blackwell](https://www.huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/sec-to-mandate-electronic-filing-of-form-144-via-edgar); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-04-12-form-144-goes-digital); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/mandatory-electronic-filing-of-form-144)
- Form 144 fields: issuer; relationship of filer (officer, director, 10% holder, immediate family); name and address of each broker; number of shares to be sold; aggregate market value; shares outstanding; approximate date of sale; securities sold in the past three months; a signed representation that the filer "does not know any material adverse information" not publicly disclosed, which — if a 10b5-1 plan was adopted — is made as of the plan adoption date, and a field "Date of plan adoption or giving of instruction, if relying on Rule 10b5-1" — [SEC Form 144](https://www.sec.gov/files/form144.pdf)
- Larcker et al. (2021) chose Form 144 because it reports adoption dates and is "not subject to voluntary reporting bias," with the caveat that Form 144 covers only restricted/affiliate resales, not purchases or unrestricted-stock sales — [SEC release 33-11138, p. ~129](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- Practitioner pages (Verity, Washington Service) discuss how to use electronic Form 144 to anticipate sales before the Form 4 arrives — [Verity Form 144 page](https://verityplatform.com/resources/form-144/); [Washington Service FAQ](https://washingtonservice.com/insights-faqs/form-144/) (both blocked; content not verified)

### Inferences
- Form 144 is filed *concurrently with or before* the sale, so it leads the Form 4 by up to several days; a Form 144 with no matching Form 4 within ~2 business days of the "approximate date of sale" is a candidate "aborted sale" event, and a Form 144 whose proposed amount is a large share of the insider's holdings (holdings from the last Form 4 column 5) is a candidate negative signal.
- The 10b5-1 adoption-date field on Form 144 has existed since well before 2023 (it is how Larcker et al. built their sample), so a 2016–present history of adoption-to-sale gaps can be built from Form 144 even for sales that pre-date the Form 4 checkbox.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of Form 144 return informativeness was found (the dedicated search was blocked by the search budget); only its use as an input dataset is documented.
- Form 144 filing thresholds (Rule 144(h): >5,000 shares or >$50,000 in any three-month period) are from general knowledge and were not verified in this session.

## KQ6. When are insider sales a negative signal?

### Takeaway
Average insider sales are uninformative, but sales are predictive when they are large relative to the insider's holdings, when several insiders sell together, when they occur in short-gap or single-trade 10b5-1 plans before earnings, and — importantly — when insiders who normally trade go *silent*: the "silence" portfolio earns a −7.3% buy-and-hold abnormal return and an insider-silence strategy yields ~7.4%/year (Gao, Ma, Ng & Wu, *Management Science* 2022), while sudden silence after a routine selling pattern predicts *positive* returns (Hong & Li, *JFQA* 2019).

### Cited Findings
- "Some Insider Sales Are Positive Signals," *Financial Analysts Journal* 60(3) (2004), data 1987–2002: only sales that are large in volume *and* a large percentage of the insider's holdings predict significantly negative abnormal returns; shares sold as a percentage of shares owned is significantly correlated with future returns — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v60.n3.2620); [PGIM PDF](https://cdn.pficdn.com/cms/pgimquantsolutions/sites/default/files/static_files/pdf/Insider_Sales_-_FAJ.pdf)
- Early work suggesting sales predict negative returns was overturned by studies with better expected-return controls (JMZ 2003: sales insignificant) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v85y2003i2p453-471.html)
- Biggerstaff, Cicero & Wintoki, "Insider trading patterns," *Journal of Corporate Finance* (2020): studies sequences of trades over time (e.g., repeated sales) as a channel insiders use to trade on longer-lived information — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0929119920300985); [Wichita State PDF (2016 WP)](https://www.wichita.edu/academics/business/accountancy/documents/InsiderTradingPatternsWintokiNov2016.pdf) (effect sizes not retrieved)
- Gao, Ma, Ng & Wu, "The Sound of Silence: What Do We Know When Insiders Do Not Trade?", *Management Science* (2022; earlier Cornell WP 2013): rational insiders neither sell (litigation risk) nor buy (bad prospects) when withholding bad news, so silence signals bad news; future returns are significantly lower after silence than after net selling; the silence portfolio's BHAR is a significant −7.3%, and the silence-minus-sell gap grows with holding period; an insider-silence-based portfolio yields ~7.36%/year excess; the gap is wider at firms with worse information environments and higher litigation risk; two quasi-natural experiments on litigation-risk law changes confirm the mechanism — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4113); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2167998); [Cornell WP PDF](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/1813/70909/1/2013_Gao_Sound_of_silence.pdf)
- Hong & Li, "The Information Content of Sudden Insider Silence," *JFQA* (2019): insiders strategically go silent when holding private information; silence following a routine *selling* schedule predicts positive abnormal returns and earnings surprises (and vice versa after routine buying); sudden silence after consecutive sells predicts a 0.22% (t = 2.72) more positive 3-day earnings-announcement CAR; a long-short strategy earns 6–10% annually (up to 10.4%); stronger where information environment is worse and arbitrage costs higher — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/information-content-of-sudden-insider-silence/32C501CD9FB2FE914771EB9C5DB2EB73); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2701254); [AEA 2017 PDF](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/9BGSNHkT)
- "Uncovering the distress anomaly: The role of insider silence and limited investor attention," *International Review of Economics & Finance* (2025) — applies silence to distressed firms — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1059056025001546) (effect sizes not retrieved)
- 10b5-1 red-flag sales (short cooling-off, single-trade plans, pre-earnings adoption) "avoid significant losses and foreshadow considerable stock price declines well in excess of industry peers" (see KQ3) — [Harvard Corp Gov Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/01/28/gaming-the-system-three-red-flags-of-potential-10b5-1-abuse/)
- Practitioner heuristic: three or more insiders selling within the same two-week period is the strongest negative share-price signal — [Alpha-Suite blog](https://alpha-suite.org/blog/insider-selling-when-to-worry) (practitioner; no sample)
- Verity flags "cessation of selling" and sell-inflections vs a 90-day baseline as scored behaviors — [Verity InsiderScore](https://verityplatform.com/solution/veritydata/insiderscore/)

### Inferences
- A sales-based short/underweight thesis should require at least two of: (i) sale ≥ X% of the insider's post-transaction holdings (Form 4 column 5), (ii) ≥2 distinct insiders selling within 7–14 days, (iii) non-plan (checkbox unchecked) discretionary S code, (iv) plan sale in a red-flag configuration (first trade in days 90–120 after adoption, single-trade plan, adoption just before earnings).
- "Insider silence" requires a *routine* history to define expectation (Hong & Li) or a firm-level net-trading state (Gao et al.); both are computable from Form 4 time series and are the strongest documented negative signals that do not rely on sales at all.
- The 2023 rule's gift-on-Form-4 requirement adds a fourth negative sub-signal: large code-G dispositions, particularly by CEOs to family foundations.

### Gaps
- Authors of the FAJ 2004 paper and the precise holding-percentage threshold were not captured.
- No post-2023 study found on cluster *selling* specifically.

## KQ7. Interactions with other signals (short interest, buybacks, 10-K tone)

### Takeaway
Insider buying is most informative when it conflicts with short sellers (≈1.4% higher next-quarter BHAR when insiders buy into a short-interest spike, per Wang 2023 *JBFA*), but one working paper warns that purchases *after* short-interest spikes reverse over a year; insider net buying before repurchase announcements predicts announcement and long-run returns (Cziraki, Lyandres & Michaely 2021 *JCF*); and 10-K disclosure tone predicts both the insider purchase ratio and abnormal returns (Sandulescu 2015).

### Cited Findings
- Wang (& Zheng), "Whose opinion matters when insiders disagree with short sellers?", *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting* (2023), 30 years of US firm-months: firms with a disagreement (insider buying + short-selling spike) earn positive abnormal returns within 3 months and a BHAR 1.438% higher in the next quarter than firms with neither; insider buying signals private information short sellers do not anticipate — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbfa.12666); [Edinburgh PDF](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/312738496/WangSZhengL2022JBFAWhoseOpinionMatters.pdf)
- "Insider Purchases after Short Interest Spikes: a False Signaling Device?" (AEA 2017 WP): CARs rise short-run but fall significantly long-run; such firms have low abnormal returns in the subsequent year — [AEA PDF](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/Dfizas4D)
- Rui (2007 EFM WP), "Short Interest, Insider Trading, and Stock Returns": insider purchases mitigate the negative returns associated with high short interest — [EFMA PDF](https://efmaefm.org/0efmsymposium/2007/papers/rui.pdf)
- Bargeron et al. (2018 WP), "What is revealed when firms repurchase against short selling?": firms repurchasing against short sellers earn positive abnormal returns, report better 8-K news and earnings — [AEA PDF](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2018/preliminary/paper/Dszzrrh3)
- Cziraki, Lyandres & Michaely (2021), "What do insiders know? Evidence from insider trading around share repurchases and SEOs," *Journal of Corporate Finance* 66: insider net buying before repurchase announcements predicts announcement returns and long-term abnormal returns, better post-event operating performance, reduced undervaluation and lower post-event cost of capital — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/corfin/v66y2021ics0929119918308824.html); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2732969)
- Sandulescu (2015): net 10-K disclosure tone predicts the insider purchase ratio (purchases/(purchases+sales)) and abnormal returns after controlling for past purchases, volatility and firm characteristics (attribution from search summary; underlying URL not confirmed) — [search-listed source](https://tuck.dartmouth.edu/uploads/content/insider_sentiment_stock_returns.pdf)
- "Insider trading and anomalies" (*Journal of Empirical Finance*, 2025): the fraction of anomaly long-leg stocks being bought by insiders forecasts anomaly returns in- and out-of-sample — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092753982500088X)
- CMP (2012) note that opportunistic trades are concentrated in poorly governed firms, suggesting governance scores interact with the signal — [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/apr11/decoding-inside-information)

### Inferences
- "Insider buys into a short squeeze" is tradeable at a 1–3 month horizon but should be exited before 12 months per the AEA 2017 reversal finding.
- Pairing opportunistic insider purchases with announced buybacks (8-K/10-Q repurchase authorization text) is supported by the Cziraki et al. mechanism; pairing with negative 10-K tone is a contrarian confirmation test (insiders buying despite dour management language).

### Gaps
- The Sandulescu 2015 citation could not be verified (journal, sample, effect size unknown).
- No study found combining Form 4 text specifically with 10-K text.

## KQ8. 2022–2026 ML/NLP work on Form 4, and what data vendors classify

### Takeaway
Recent work applies gradient boosting and LLM-era tooling to Form 4 (a Feb 2026 arXiv paper on microcap purchase signals; a 2025 arXiv dataset of >1M transactions for late-filing detection; a 2023 *Scientific Data* dataset that exposes footnote text), but no retrieved study uses LLMs on footnote text to forecast returns; vendors (Verity/InsiderScore, 2iQ, InsiderBrief, Form4API) converge on the same classifications: P-only buys, discretionary non-plan S, cluster (2+ insiders, 7 days), trade size vs holdings and vs the insider's own history, and exclusion of A/F/M/G mechanical rows.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The vendor consensus is a reasonable baseline feature set; the under-exploited inputs are (a) footnote text categories (KQ4), (b) the post-2023 checkbox/adoption-date/Item 408 fields (KQ3), (c) electronic Form 144 (KQ5), and (d) silence features (KQ6). ## KQ9. Practical data: Form 4 XML, transaction codes, timing, derivative vs non-derivative tables, and evidence on decay

### Takeaway
Form 4 must be filed before the end of the second business day after the transaction; EDGAR serves it as XML with `nonDerivativeTable` (Table I) and `derivativeTable` (Table II), transaction codes in `transactionCoding`, a direct/indirect flag with a nature-of-ownership text, and footnotes linked by `footnoteId` IDREFs; the general purchase anomaly has weakened in 2018–2023 samples (and ~50% post-publication decay is the cross-anomaly norm), while conditional signals (opportunistic, cluster, indirect, silence, 10b5-1 red flags) are the ones with recent supporting evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Filing deadline: Form 4 "must be filed before the end of the second business day following the day on which the subject transaction has been executed"; execution date vs. "deemed execution date" rules in General Instruction 4 — [SEC Form 4 instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form4.pdf)
- Table I (non-derivative) vs Table II (derivative): report exercise/conversion of a derivative in Table II as a disposition of the derivative and in Table I as acquisition of the underlying; puts/calls/options/warrants/convertibles only in Table II; each transaction on a separate line; code V flags voluntary early reporting — [SEC Form 4 instructions, Instruction 4](https://www.sec.gov/files/form4.pdf)
- Transaction codes (Form 4 Instruction 8): General — P (open market or private purchase), S (open market or private sale), V (voluntarily reported earlier than required); Rule 16b-3 — A (grant/award), D (disposition to issuer), F (payment of exercise price or tax liability by delivering or withholding securities), I (discretionary transaction under 16b-3(f)), M (exercise/conversion of derivative exempt under 16b-3); Derivative — C (conversion), E (expiration of short derivative), H (expiration/cancellation of long derivative with value received), O (exercise of out-of-the-money derivative), X (exercise of in/at-the-money derivative); Other 16(b)-exempt/small acquisition — G (bona fide gift), L (small acquisition under 16a-6), W (acquisition/disposition by will or laws of descent), Z (deposit into or withdrawal from voting trust); Other — J (other acquisition or disposition, describe), K (equity swap or similar), U (disposition in a tender offer in a change of control) — [SEC Form 4 instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form4.pdf) (G/L/W/Z are from the "Other Section 16(b) Exempt Transaction and Small Acquisition Codes" block; the heading was captured, full lines partially)
- Rule 10b5-1(c) indication: checkbox plus adoption date in Explanation of Responses (Instruction 10) — [SEC Form 4 instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form4.pdf)
- EDGAR Ownership XML Technical Specification (versions 2–4) defines Forms 3/4/5 and amendments; Form 4 XML holds `nonDerivativeTable` and `derivativeTable`; footnotes are linked to fields via ID/IDREF (`footnoteId` elements referencing F1, F2, …) — [SEC spec v4 draft](https://www.sec.gov/info/edgar/ownershipxmltechspec-v4_d.htm); [SEC spec v3 PDF](https://www.sec.gov/info/edgar/ownershipxmltechspec-v3.pdf); [example Form 4 XML](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/809398/000120919105009166/doc5.xml)
- Vendor data dictionaries describe the same structure (reporting owner relationship, transaction coding, ownership nature, footnotes) — [sec-api.io Form 4 dataset](https://sec-api.io/datasets/form-4)
- Decay evidence: McLean & Pontiff (*JF* 2016) find average post-publication decline of ~50% across anomalies (US-specific, driven by trader awareness) — [HEC PDF](https://www.hec.ca/finance/Fichier/McLean.pdf); [SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2080900)
- Recent US Form 4 sample (Nov 2018–Nov 2023, 58,732 filings): lower short-horizon abnormal returns than earlier studies; vanish under capacity limits; placebo concerns (FRL 2024) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612324015435)
- Pre- vs post-Sarbanes-Oxley: five-day announcement abnormal return to insider purchases rose from 1.0% to 2.3% after SOX shortened the filing deadline to two business days (from a summary of the literature) — [Insider Signals Substack summary](https://insidersignals.substack.com/p/insider-trading-signals-following) (secondary)
- "Insider trading patterns during the COVID period" (*Pacific-Basin Finance Journal*, 2025) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X2500294X) (not retrieved)

### Inferences
- Construction recipe: parse EDGAR full-index for form type "4"/"4/A"; load the XML; keep Table I rows; build features = {code, sharesTraded, price, postTransactionShares, D/I flag, natureOfOwnership text, footnote texts, 10b5-1 checkbox, reporting-owner relationship/title, transactionDate, filingDate}; compute insider-level histories (routine flag, pre-EA profitability, prior 12-month trade count); compute firm-level cluster counts in 7-day windows; signal date = EDGAR acceptance timestamp (filingDate), not transactionDate; enter at next open after acceptance; hold 1–3 months for purchase signals, 12 months for gift/silence signals.

## KQ10. Synthesis: candidate testable theses from Form 4 and related filings

### Takeaway
Ten concrete theses follow from the evidence above; the first five have peer-reviewed support with stated effect sizes, the rest are supported by regulatory text plus practitioner or working-paper evidence and are better suited to in-house backtests.

### Cited Findings
- (T1) Opportunistic-insider purchases, value-weighted long-short: 82 bp/month (CMP 2012, 1989–2007 era) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01740.x)
- (T2) Insiders with profitable pre-earnings trading histories: four-factor alpha >1%/month (Ali & Hirshleifer 2017) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X17302349)
- (T3) Cluster purchases (2+ insiders within 2–7 days): ~2.1% next-month abnormal, +0.9 pp vs solo (Alldredge & Blank 2019); ~3.8% vs 2.0% over 21 trading days (2iQ) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfir.12172); [2iQ](https://www.2iqresearch.com/blog/what-is-cluster-buying-and-why-is-it-such-a-powerful-insider-signal)
- (T4) Indirect-account (trust/family/foundation) trades: +0.90%/month hedge return, +50 bp vs direct; 6.34% over 2 years (JFQA) — [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7933F3093D5055F73128AF294FFF0945/S0022109022001119a.pdf/indirect-insider-trading.pdf)
- (T5) Insider silence: −7.3% BHAR for silence portfolio; ~7.4%/yr strategy (Gao et al. 2022); 6–10%/yr for sudden-silence long-short (Hong & Li 2019) — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4113); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/information-content-of-sudden-insider-silence/32C501CD9FB2FE914771EB9C5DB2EB73)
- (T6) Gifts (code G) as a negative signal: ~−4% over the following year; ~−3% within a month for CEO foundation gifts (Avci et al. 2021; Yermack 2009); now reported on Form 4 within 2 business days — [SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3795537); [NYU PDF](https://web-docs.stern.nyu.edu/glucksman/docs/Gifts_20081231.pdf); [SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)
- (T7) 10b5-1 sales timed to the 90–120-day post-adoption window / single-trade plans / pre-earnings adoption: pre-reform losses avoided (Larcker et al. 2021); post-reform migration and rising dollar profits (Bergen/CEPR) vs flat returns (JAE 2026) — [SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf); [Verity](https://verityplatform.com/resources/study-insider-sales-10b5-1/); [CLS Blue Sky](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2025/07/31/insider-trading-after-the-2022-rule-10b5-1-amendment/)
- (T8) CFO purchases over CEO purchases: +5 pp 12-month excess (Wang, Shin & Francis 2012) — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/are-cfos-trades-more-informative-than-ceos-trades/B7DD71285F1326E694CC860193C87710)
- (T9) Insider buying against short-interest spikes: +1.438% next-quarter BHAR (Wang 2023), with 12-month reversal risk (AEA 2017 WP) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbfa.12666); [AEA PDF](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/Dfizas4D)
- (T10) Large sales relative to holdings by multiple insiders: negative abnormal returns (FAJ 2004; practitioner cluster-sell rule) — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v60.n3.2620)

### Inferences
- Footnote parsing is the enabling step for T4, T6, T7 and for cleaning T1–T3, T8, T10 (removing F/M/A rows and plan sales); the XML already links each row to its footnote via `footnoteId`, so an LLM pass over "Explanation of Responses" is cheap relative to the data volume (~3,000 insiders reported gifts alone in 2021 per the SEC).
- Where decay is documented: unconditional purchase-following (FRL 2024), and possibly 10b5-1 red flags if the JAE finding holds. Where persistence is documented: silence (2019–2022 publications), indirect trades (2022), cluster buys (vendor claims reduced-but-positive), and 10b5-1 window-timing (2025–2026 Bergen result).
