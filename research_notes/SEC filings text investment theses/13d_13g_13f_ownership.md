## Key Question 1: Hedge-fund activism via Schedule 13D — announcement returns, long-run performance, and whether Item 4 stated objectives predict different outcomes

### Takeaway
A 13D filing by an activist hedge fund is associated with a ~5-8% abnormal return in a ±10 to ±20 day window with no reversal over 1-5 years on an equal-weighted basis; the effect is concentrated in small caps (~9% smallest tercile vs 2-3% largest terciles) and is largely driven by targets that end up acquired. Stated objectives in Item 4 matter: "sale of company / strategic alternatives" and governance/board demands carry the largest returns, while vague "undervalued, may talk to management" filings carry the smallest; value-weighted long-run returns are statistically zero.

### Cited Findings
- Brav, Jiang, Partnoy & Thomas (2008), Journal of Finance 63(4):1729-1775, sample of 1,059 hedge-fund activism events 2001-2006: abnormal return around announcement of ~7% with no reversal in the subsequent year; activists attained success or partial success in two-thirds of cases; targets saw higher payout, operating performance and CEO turnover — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x); [ECGI working-paper PDF](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalbravjiangthomaspartnoy.pdf) (search summary). ### Inferences
- Thesis 1A (core 13D event trade): Buy on the EDGAR acceptance of an initial SC 13D where (i) the filer is a hedge fund / known activist (SharkWatch 50 or Insightia top-10 proxy; cover-page type IA/PN/HC with Item 3 "working capital of funds"), (ii) Item 5(c) shows an open-market tabular trade history (the SEC's "non-corporate-action" marker), and (iii) the filer appears to be still accumulating (stake growth across the 60-day table; large last-week trades). Expected CAR(-20,+20) in the 6-17% range by accumulation status per SEC Tables 5-6, with roughly 3% of it arriving between the old deadline and the day after filing. Post-filing drift exists but most of the move is within ±1 day of acceptance, so execution at acceptance time (10 p.m. ET cutoff; most filings arrive during the trading day) matters.
- Thesis 1B (objective-conditioned): Weight toward Item 4 language that names a specific outcome (sale/strategic alternatives, board seats, capital return) and away from boilerplate ("acquired for investment purposes ... may engage in discussions"). Greenwood-Schor show the long-run alpha is almost entirely in targets that are eventually acquired; Fos (proxy contests) and Boyson-Mooradian show business-strategy/undervaluation and governance+excess-cash demands pay most; capital-structure-only demands pay less.
- Thesis 1C (size tilt / value-weighted caveat): The headline 7% is an equal-weighted small-cap phenomenon. Expect 2-3% in large caps and no detectable value-weighted long-run alpha (deHaan et al.). Capacity is therefore limited; SEC's own estimate of average value creation per campaign ($36M-$222M) implies small position sizes.
- Decay/crowding: the SEC's 2011-2021 staff sample (CAR(-20,+20) 5.7% for completed-stake filers) is consistent with Brav et al.'s 1994-2018 ~5%, i.e., the announcement effect has not disappeared; but the count of initial 13Ds halved after 2010, and the 2024 deadline cut compresses the pre-filing drift (see KQ5). Greenwood-Schor imply the long-run leg is cyclical with M&A conditions.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full text of Brav et al. 2008, BBJ 2015, Greenwood-Schor, Klein-Zur, or Boyson-Mooradian, so per-objective announcement-return tables (e.g., Brav 2008 Table showing returns by "sale of company" vs "general undervaluation") and t-statistics are not recorded. Report writer should pull Brav et al. (2008) Table IV/V and Greenwood-Schor Table 3 for exact per-category CARs.
- No 2020-2026 peer-reviewed update of 13D announcement returns found beyond the SEC's 2011-2021 staff figures and Brav et al. 2022 (1994-2018).

## Key Question 2: Text analysis of 13D Item 4 and activist letters (tone, demands, hostility, specificity), wolf packs, 13D/A amendments and settlements

### Takeaway
Text in Item 4 and in 13D exhibits/voluntary disclosures carries information beyond the ownership number: campaigns that state demands publicly (open activism; board-seat demands; voluntary letters) earn higher announcement returns and win more often, and settlements (filed as 13D/A exhibits) are met with positive reactions and followed by the changes sought. The literature here is thin, mostly 2020-2024, and only one paper (McDonough, Nagar & Schoenfeld 2024) uses an LLM.

### Cited Findings
- Aiken & Lee (2020), Journal of Corporate Finance, "Let's talk sooner rather than later: The strategic communication decisions of activist blockholders": uses Item 4 language describing ongoing or past negotiations to flag whether the activist communicated with the target before the 13D; nearly a quarter of campaigns begin as "open activism" (demands stated in the 13D); more credible, lower-cost activists engage early; the initial communication choice is related to eventual merger outcomes; operational improvements are more likely in open-activism campaigns and the market responds positively after the 13D anticipating them — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0929119920300377); [SSRN 3260577](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3260577); [URI repository](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/cba_facpubs/285/) (search summary).
- McDonough, Nagar & Schoenfeld (2024), Review of Accounting Studies 29(3), "Voluntary disclosures by activist investors: the role of activist expectations": hand-collected full texts of activist disclosures; topics generated with a large language model; disclosures are accompanied by positive abnormal returns, lower bid-ask spreads and higher future earnings vs. similar targets without voluntary activist disclosure; disclosures demanding a board seat (the most common demand) have the highest announcement returns; disclosers win proxy contests and directorships more often than non-disclosers — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-024-09836-6); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID4710634_code1695805.pdf?abstractid=3137216); [BFI working-paper version "Voluntary Investor Disclosure"](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Venky-Nagar_Voluntary-Investor-Disclosure5.pdf) (search summary).

### Inferences
- Thesis 2A (specificity score): Build a classifier over Item 4 text with labels {boilerplate investment-purpose; private engagement mentioned; explicit demand: board seats / sale / capital return / operational}. Evidence (Aiken-Lee; McDonough et al.) says explicit board-seat demands and open demands earn the highest announcement returns and higher win rates; boilerplate filings (which the SEC found are common on 13D) should be down-weighted or treated like 13Gs.
- Thesis 2B (settlement signal): Monitor SC 13D/A filings whose Item 4 or Item 6 references a "cooperation agreement"/"settlement agreement" with an EX-99 agreement attached, and 8-K Item 1.01/5.02 with the same; Bebchuk et al. 2020 document positive reactions and subsequent payout/CEO-turnover changes. Also track standstill expiry dates in those agreements (the Franklin Street example expires Dec 31, 2025 or 30 days before the 2026 nomination deadline) as a calendar of possible re-escalation.
- Thesis 2C (wolf-pack detection): Abnormal volume on the day the lead activist crosses 5% (Wong 2020 proxy) and a subsequent cluster of 13Ds/13Gs from other hedge funds predict a 5.5-8.4% higher campaign BHAR and ~14% announcement returns internationally; practical implementation uses Item 5(c) trade dates to back out the crossing date and compares to volume.
- Hostility/tone: no retrieved paper directly links a tone score of 13D letters to returns. Koo et al. (2026) link hostile tactics to target resistance (management-journal framing), which suggests hostile tone may raise contest probability rather than returns per se.

### Gaps
- No study found that scores 13D Item 4 tone/hostility with NLP and reports a return spread; the ProMarket-summarized paper's authors and effect sizes were not recoverable.
- The exact announcement-return magnitudes in McDonough-Nagar-Schoenfeld (by topic), Aiken-Lee (open vs private), and Bebchuk et al. 2020 (settlement CAR) were not retrievable from abstracts; the writer should pull them from the papers.
- Could not verify whether Item 4 narrative text is now a structured XML field (see KQ8) — this determines how easy bulk extraction is post-Dec 2024.

## Key Question 3: 13G vs 13D — passive 13G filings by hedge funds, 13G-to-13D switches, and the role of passive (index) owners

### Takeaway
A 13G by a hedge fund earns a small but reliably positive announcement return (0.8% to 1.6% in 3-5 day windows; ~1.2% over (-30,+10)) and is followed by operating improvements; a 13G-to-13D switch is a change-of-intent event that practitioners claim produces larger reactions than fresh 13Ds, but no peer-reviewed effect size for switches was retrievable. Higher passive (index) ownership raises activists' odds of winning board seats or a sale, so passive-ownership share is a useful conditioning variable.

### Cited Findings
- Edmans, Fang & Zur (2013), Review of Financial Studies 26(6):1443-1482, "The Effect of Liquidity on Governance": 13G filings by hedge funds have an average CAR of 0.8% over (-1,+1) (SEC fn. 883) and are followed by operating-performance improvements (SEC fn. 906); liquidity raises the propensity to file 13G rather than 13D, and 13G announcement returns are larger in liquid firms — [SEC Release 33-11253](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf); [NBER w17567](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17567/w17567.pdf); [author PDF](https://alexedmans.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Liquidity.pdf) (search summary).
- Clifford (2008), Journal of Corporate Finance 14:323, "Value Creation or Destruction? Hedge Funds as Shareholder Activists": hedge-fund 13G filings earn 1.6% CAR over (-2,+2), similar whether filed within 10 days of the trigger or later — [SEC Release 33-11253 fn. 883](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Albuquerque, Fos & Schroth (2022): hedge-fund 13G CAR ~1.2% over (-30,+10); 13D 6.34% vs 13G 0.59% in their comparison; 74.8% of the 13D return attributed to activism rather than stock selection — [SEC Release 33-11253 fn. 883](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X21003950) (search summary).
- SEC interpretation of 13G returns: researchers "vary in their interpretation," attributing it to governance, stock-picking ability, or the presence of a significant shareholder "known to pursue activist strategies at some of the issuers in which they invest" — [SEC Release 33-11253 fn. 883](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Giglia (2016), "A Little Letter, A Big Difference: An Empirical Inquiry into Possible Misuse of Schedule 13G/13D Filings," 116 Columbia Law Review (student note) — examines activist hedge funds filing 13G then switching to 13D — [Columbia Law Review](https://www.columbialawreview.org/content/a-little-letter-a-big-difference-an-empirical-inquiry-into-possible-misuse-of-schedule-13g13d-filings/) (search summary; effect sizes not retrievable).
- Appel, Gormley & Keim, "Standing on the Shoulders of Giants: The Effect of Passive Investors on Activism" (NBER w22707; RFS): higher passive mutual-fund ownership is associated with more proxy fights, more board representation, more sales of the target and removal of takeover defenses; example: ValueAct won a Microsoft board seat with <1% — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22707/w22707.pdf); [Harvard Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/?p=72759) (search summary).
- Practitioner claim: "Reclassifications from 13G to 13D produce larger reactions than fresh 13D filings, because the change in intent is the entire signal — the position was already known" — [13finsight explainer](https://13finsight.com/learn/13d-vs-13g-filings-active-vs-passive-intent-explainer) (search summary; no source study given). Another practitioner page cites "market-adjusted returns of 1.64% for passive filings" — [Databento](https://databento.com/compliance/schedule-13g) (search summary; likely Clifford 2008's 1.6%).
- Switching mechanics: a 13G filer that loses eligibility must file a 13D within 10 days (Rule 13d-1(e)) and, during the cooling-off period, cannot vote the shares or buy more — [SEC C&DI page](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/compliance-disclosure-interpretations/exchange-act-sections-13d-13g-regulation-13d-g-beneficial-ownership-reporting); practitioner descriptions at [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/sec-settlement-highlights-risks-for-13g-filers-when-moving-from-passive-to-active-status) and [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/securities/714412/when-passive-investors-drift-into-activist-status) (search summary). Note: the 2023 amendments cut the post-ineligibility 13D deadline to five business days (see KQ5).
- 2025 C&DI 103.12: a 13G filer that "calls for asset sales, restructuring, director slate changes, or compensation reform" or conditions director support on management changes may lose 13G eligibility; merely discussing views and how they inform voting does not — [SEC C&DIs, Reg 13D-G](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/compliance-disclosure-interpretations/exchange-act-sections-13d-13g-regulation-13d-g-beneficial-ownership-reporting).
- SEC Table 4 (initial 13Gs in 2022): 8,433 initial 13Gs and 18,090 amendments from 4,321 lead filers; QIIs 4,660 filings (median 40 days trigger-to-filing, median 6% stake), Exempt investors 1,508 (45 days, 15%), Passive investors (Rule 13d-1(c)) 2,222 (10 days, 6%) — [SEC Release 33-11253](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Filer identification on cover page: Item 12 "Type of Reporting Person" codes seen in practice: IA (investment adviser), PN (partnership), OO (other), IN (individual), HC (holding company/parent), CO (corporation); hedge-fund groups typically file as PN + IA/OO + IN — [Franklin Street 13D/A example](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031316/000090266424006808/p24-3453sc13da.htm). 13G filers must indicate the rule relied on (13d-1(b) institutional QII, 13d-1(c) passive, 13d-1(d) exempt) — [SEC C&DIs](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/compliance-disclosure-interpretations/exchange-act-sections-13d-13g-regulation-13d-g-beneficial-ownership-reporting).

### Inferences
- Thesis 3A (activist-13G watchlist): A Rule 13d-1(c) "passive" 13G by a filer that is on the SharkWatch 50 / Insightia list, or that has historically converted 13Gs to 13Ds, is a lower-magnitude (~1-1.6%) but earlier signal than a 13D; the 2024 rule moved passive-investor initial 13Gs to five business days, so it is now nearly as timely as a 13D.
- Thesis 3B (13G-to-13D switch): Treat a SC 13D filed by a CIK that previously filed SC 13G on the same subject CIK as a distinct event class; the trade is executed at acceptance; the 10-day (now 5-business-day) cooling-off bars the filer from buying, so no further filer-driven demand is coming in that window. Effect size is undocumented in peer-reviewed work found here — it needs to be estimated in-house.
- Thesis 3C (conditioning on passive ownership): Combine 13D events with the target's index-fund ownership share (from 13F / N-PORT); Appel-Gormley-Keim and Kedia et al. imply higher expected success and 7.8-15.5% higher 36-month BHAR per SD of activism-friendly ownership.
- Interpretation caveat: the positive 13G reaction is partly a stock-picking signal (Albuquerque et al. attribute ~25% of the 13D return to stock selection), so 13G filings by non-activist hedge funds with strong track records may also carry information.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate of returns around 13G-to-13D switches was retrievable (Giglia 2016 may contain descriptive statistics; blocked).
- Appel-Gormley-Keim effect sizes (percentage-point increases in success probability) not captured.

## Key Question 4: Form 13F — best-ideas/copycat strategies, the 45-day lag, confidential treatment and restatements

### Takeaway
13F-based "best ideas" (highest active-weight positions) and hedge-fund consensus/conviction portfolios earn published alphas of roughly 1.6-2.1% per quarter (Cohen-Polk-Silli) or 2.8-4.5% per year (Antón-Cohen-Polk) even after the 45-day lag; confidential (delayed) 13F holdings and restated holdings carry additional alpha; but hedge-fund security-selection alpha in 13F data declined markedly after 2008 and copycat alpha is sensitive to which managers are cloned. 13F is fully structured (no narrative text), so text analysis adds nothing beyond the confidential-treatment and amendment flags.

### Cited Findings
- Cohen, Polk & Silli, "Best Ideas" (NBER 2008 conference version): active managers' "best ideas" (largest deviation from benchmark weight) outperform the market by ~1.6-2.1% per quarter depending on benchmark — [NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2008/bff08/polk.pdf); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/alpha-cloning-following-13f-fillings) (search summary).
- Antón, Cohen & Polk, "Best Ideas" (HBS WP 21-004; LSE PDF): best ideas identified by portfolio weights outperform by 2.8-4.5% per year; investing only in best ideas can roughly double the Sharpe ratio — [HBS PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=21-004.pdf); [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/bestideas.pdf); [HBS page](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=58365) (search summary).
- Angelini, Iqbal & Jivraj, "Systematic 13F Hedge Fund Alpha" (Imperial / Lancaster FoFI 2020): a strategy built on conviction and consensus measures from hedge-fund 13Fs delivers a six-factor alpha of 0.32% per month and Sharpe 0.75; alpha drops to -0.003% (insignificant) when tracking certain other manager categories — [Lancaster PDF](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2020/files/2020/04/FoFI-2020-090-Farouk-Jivraj.pdf); [Imperial Spiral](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/59bae988-0303-450f-8b11-62ebe5d8cb03) (search summary).
- Schroeder (2025), "Outperforming the Market: Portfolio Strategy Cloning from SEC 13F Filings" (SSRN 5399672): cloned top-quartile portfolios from >150,000 13F portfolios 2013-2023 replicate fund performance and beat the S&P 500 — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5399672) (search summary).
- Agarwal, Jiang, Tang & Yang (2013), Journal of Finance 68:739-783, "Uncovering Hedge Fund Skill from the Portfolio Holdings They Hide": confidential holdings (disclosed late via 13F amendments, excluded from standard databases) earn positive abnormal returns; funds with large, risky, nonconventional portfolios request confidentiality more; motive is protecting private information and avoiding front-running, not window dressing — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12012); [SMU copy](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5601&context=lkcsb_research) (search summary). Companion: Aragon, Hertzel & Shi, "Why Do Hedge Funds Avoid Disclosure? Evidence from Confidential 13F Filings," JFQA — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/why-do-hedge-funds-avoid-disclosure-evidence-from-confidential-13f-filings/D26B3BC0EF07E387B4E80519565C33E8) (search summary).
- "Do Hedge Funds Strategically Misreport Their Holdings? Evidence from 13F Restatements" (Management Science 2024): restated holdings earn significant abnormal returns, implying some originals are misreported to hide trading intentions; restatements facilitate copycat trading and price convergence — [INFORMS](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.08833); [Notre Dame PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/Restatement.pdf) (search summary).
- Decay: a twenty-year analysis of hedge-fund 13F holdings finds historically abnormal security-selection returns but "a significant decline in this abnormal return after the 2008 financial crisis"; a drop in fund performance after a fund begins filing 13F and higher return correlations within style — [Anderson (2018), Journal of Financial Research, "An Examination of 13F Filings"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfir.12150); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/alpha-cloning-following-13f-fillings) (search summary).
- Jiang & Sun / Huang (2024) JFR "Informed trading by hedge funds" and Jiao, Massa & Zhang (2016) JFE 122(3):544-567 "Short selling meets hedge fund 13F: An anatomy of informed demand" — 13F long demand combined with short-interest data identifies informed demand — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X16301520); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfir.12386) (search summary; effect sizes not captured).
- Shive & Yun (2013), JFE 107:220, "Are Mutual Funds Sitting Ducks?": hedge funds trade on expected mutual-fund flows; anticipatory trading strengthened after 2004 quarterly disclosure — [SEC Release 33-11253 fn. 905](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Rules: managers with investment discretion over $100 million or more in Section 13(f) securities file 13F-HR (holdings), 13F-NT (notice), or 13F-HR/A and 13F-NT/A amendments (restatement or newly added holdings) within 45 days after calendar-quarter end; confidential treatment is available for risk-arbitrage or similar commercial information for 3-12 months (extendable) and indefinitely for personal holdings; since Feb 28, 2023 requests go through EDGAR form type 13F-CTR; since Jan 3, 2023 values are in whole dollars — [SEC 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13ffaq.htm). The information table has issuer name, class, CUSIP, FIGI, value, shares, sole/shared/none investment discretion and voting authority; the cover page has a confidential-treatment box — [Form 13F PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/form13f.pdf).
- SEC structured 13F data sets: SUBMISSION, COVERPAGE and INFOTABLE tables extracted from the XML portion, updated quarterly after each due date — [SEC 13F data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets).

### Inferences
- Thesis 4A (best-ideas clone): each quarter after the 45-day deadline (mid-Feb/May/Aug/Nov), take each qualifying manager's top active-weight positions (weight minus benchmark/market weight, or weight relative to the manager's own average), aggregate across a curated manager set (concentrated, long-biased hedge funds with persistent alpha), hold one quarter. Published alpha 2.8-4.5%/yr equal-weighted; expect lower post-2008 and sensitivity to the manager set (Angelini et al. show alpha vanishes for some categories).
- Thesis 4B (confidential and restated holdings): parse 13F-HR/A filings and 13F-CTR outcomes; new holdings appearing in amendments (previously confidential) and restated positions have earned positive abnormal returns (Agarwal et al.; the Management Science restatement paper). These are small, idiosyncratic samples but structurally under-covered by data vendors.
- Thesis 4C (13F x 13D linkage): identify hedge funds with activist histories (13D filers) whose new 13F positions are 3-4.9% of a company (just under the 13D threshold) — a candidate list for future 13Ds/wolf-pack formation; no paper found testing this precisely (gap).
- Text limit: 13F has no narrative; the only non-numeric signals are the confidential-treatment flag, amendment type (restatement vs new holdings), and the "other managers" cross-references.

### Gaps
- Jiang, Verbeek & Wang (holdings-based fund-skill measures) could not be located; Cohen-Polk-Silli published version (RFS?) not confirmed. Quantpedia's out-of-sample tracking of its alpha-cloning strategy was blocked.
- Exact t-stats and post-publication decay figures for best-ideas strategies not retrievable.

## Key Question 5: The 2023/2024 SEC amendments to 13D/13G (deadlines, 10 p.m. cut-off, XML) and what they do to the trading window

### Takeaway
Effective Feb 5, 2024 the initial 13D deadline went from 10 calendar days to five business days and 13D amendments from "promptly" to two business days; 13G deadlines moved to quarterly (45 days after quarter-end) with faster triggers; the filing day cut-off moved from 5:30 p.m. to 10 p.m. ET; XML became mandatory Dec 18, 2024. SEC staff estimated this pulls forward ~3% of abnormal return that previously accrued between day 7 and the filing day, i.e., less pre-filing accumulation for activists and a shorter window for front-runners/tippees, while making all cover-page and item data machine-readable.

### Cited Findings
- Deadlines (old → new) from the final rule's summary table: Schedule 13D initial: within 10 days after acquiring >5% → within five business days; 13D amendments: "promptly" → within two business days; 13G initial for QIIs and Exempt Investors: 45 days after calendar year-end → 45 days after calendar quarter-end in which ownership exceeds 5% (QIIs: five business days after month-end if >10%); Passive investors initial: within 10 days → within five business days; all 13G amendments: 45 days after year-end if any change → 45 days after quarter-end if a material change; QII amendments on exceeding 10% or ±5%: 10 days after month-end → five business days after month-end; Passive investor amendments on exceeding 10% or ±5%: "promptly" → two business days; filing cut-off time for 13D and 13G: 5:30 p.m. ET → 10 p.m. ET (Regulation S-T Rule 13(a)(4)) — [SEC Release 33-11253, pp. 9-11](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Effective date Feb 5, 2024; compliance with revised 13G deadlines required from Sept 30, 2024 (first quarterly amendment due 45 days after Sept 30, 2024); structured-data (XML) compliance required from Dec 18, 2024 with voluntary compliance from Dec 18, 2023 — [SEC Release 33-11253, Sec. II.G](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf); [SEC press release 2023-219](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219).
- Chair Gensler: "In our fast-paced markets, it shouldn't take 10 days for the public to learn about an attempt to change or influence control of a public company"; the 10-day rule dated from the 1968 Williams Act — [SEC press release 2023-219](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219).
- The SEC did not adopt proposed Rule 13d-3(e) (cash-settled derivatives deemed beneficial ownership) or the proposed group-formation rule; instead it issued guidance that cash-settled derivatives can confer beneficial ownership under existing Rule 13d-3 in some circumstances and amended Item 6 of Schedule 13D to require disclosure of all derivative interests referencing the covered class — [SEC Release 33-11253, pp. 10-12](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Staff economic analysis: among 2011-2021 campaign-type filings, 80% of filers had already completed accumulation within five business days of crossing 5%; for the 20% still buying, median abnormal return from the new deadline to day +1 was 1.9% (3.1% if <90% accumulated; 6.9% if <75%), and estimated transfers from selling shareholders in that window were $425K-$5.1M per campaign ($23M-$26M per year in aggregate); filers that used the full 10 days saw ~3% abnormal return from day 7 to day +1 — [SEC Release 33-11253, Figures 4-5, Tables 3, 5](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Staff view on timing of the reaction: positive drift after the filing date "is consistent with ... academic studies" and researchers use windows such as (-20,+20); the SEC expects the shortened deadline to accelerate the reaction "concentrated around the actual filing date itself" rather than shift it later — [SEC Release 33-11253 fn. 737](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Structured data: 13D and 13G must be filed in XML via EDGAR Online Forms (submission types SCHEDULE 13D, SCHEDULE 13D/A, SCHEDULE 13G, SCHEDULE 13G/A replace the legacy SC 13D / SC 13D/A HTML/ASCII types); exhibits are attached as separate files; XML SC 13D/A is not yet supported when combined with SC TO-I/SC TO-T tender-offer filings (those remain non-structured) — [EDGAR Filer Manual Vol. II v72, Dec 2024, Secs. 7.3.11.2, 8.1.10-8.1.11](https://www.sec.gov/files/edgar/filermanual/archive/efmvol2-v72.pdf). The EDGAR Schedule 13D and 13G XML Technical Specification v2.1 (661 KB zip) is the schema source — [SEC tech spec v2.1](https://www.sec.gov/file/schedule-13d-13g-tech-specs-21); [v2.0](https://www.sec.gov/file/schedule-13d-13g-tech-specs-20). Vendor notes say "only the exhibits ... will remain unstructured" — [Toppan Merrill](https://www.toppanmerrill.com/blog/sec-modernizes-beneficial-ownership-reporting-on-schedule-13d-and-13g-with-xml-format/) (search summary).
- Law-firm summaries of the same changes: [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2023/10/sec-amends-rules-for-reporting-beneficial-ownership-on-schedules-13d-and-13g); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2023/10/sec-shortens-filing-deadlines-for-schedules-13d-g); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2023/10/sec-adopts-substantial-changes-to-reporting-requirements-for-significant-shareholders); [Federal Register 88 FR 76896 (Nov 7, 2023)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/07/2023-22678/modernization-of-beneficial-ownership-reporting).

### Inferences
- For an event-driven 13D strategy, the actionable public window is now the day of acceptance (which can arrive as late as 10 p.m. ET and so trade the next open) plus the documented post-filing drift; the ~3% that used to leak in days 7-10 is now largely inside the filing-day reaction. Backtests spanning 2024 should split pre/post Feb 5, 2024 and use acceptance timestamps rather than filing dates.
- Activists now report with smaller completed stakes (less time to build), so the Item 5(c) table's share-of-volume numbers and the reported percentage are less informative about final size; 13D/A amendments within two business days of ±1% changes make the post-filing accumulation path observable almost in real time — a new signal series (amendment frequency and direction) that the pre-2024 literature could not study.
- Quarterly 13G amendments (vs annual) make 13G stake changes by hedge funds a usable quarterly signal from Q4 2024 onward, complementing 13F.

### Gaps
- No post-2024 study yet measuring whether the announcement CAR or pre-filing run-up changed after the deadline cut.
- Could not open the XML technical specification zip to confirm which Items (e.g., Item 4 narrative) are typed text fields vs free text.

## Key Question 6: Other ownership-related filings with text — 13E-3 going-private, SC TO tender offers, DEFC14A/PREC14A/DFAN14A proxy-contest materials, PX14A6G exempt solicitations; returns around proxy fights and settlements

### Takeaway
Proxy contests earn ~6.5% abnormal announcement returns, with gains concentrated in contests about business strategy and undervaluation rather than capital structure or governance; settlements (Bebchuk et al. 2020) and voluntary activist disclosures (McDonough et al. 2024, which draw on DFAN14A/PX14A6G/press-release texts) are both positive events. No empirical study specific to 13E-3 or SC TO announcement returns was retrievable in this sandbox.

### Cited Findings
- Fos (2017), Management Science 63(3):655-671, "The Disciplinary Effects of Proxy Contests": hand-collected sample of all proxy contests 1994-2012; average abnormal return ~6.5% around contest announcement; contests addressing business strategy and undervaluation are most beneficial, while those targeting capital structure and governance do not raise value; targets are smaller, more liquid, with higher institutional ownership, and show poor pre-contest performance and rising entrenchment — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2340); [SSRN 1705707](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1705707); [Bauer PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/VyacheslavThe%20Disciplinary%20Effects%20of%20Proxy%20Contests.pdf) (search summary).

### Inferences
- Thesis 6A (proxy-contest objective filter): Parse PREC14A/DEFC14A/DFAN14A for the dissident's stated platform; Fos's result implies buying contests framed around strategy/undervaluation (often with a sale-process demand) and avoiding pure capital-structure/governance contests.
- Thesis 6B (settlement calendar): Settlements cluster ahead of nomination deadlines; the SC 13D/A + 8-K pair announcing a cooperation agreement is the event; expected positive CAR (magnitude not captured here).
- Thesis 6C (13E-3 fairness language): The Item 8 "fairness" statement and dissenting-director disclosure are natural text features for predicting bump-ups/appraisal, but no return study was found.

### Gaps
- No academic estimates found (within reach) for returns around SC 13E-3 or SC TO filings, PX14A6G exempt solicitations as an event class, or DFAN14A fight-letter tone; the writer should treat these as untested ideas.
- Fos's per-objective CARs and Bebchuk et al.'s settlement CAR magnitudes not captured.

## Key Question 7: 2022-2026 LLM-based work on 13D or activist-letter text

### Takeaway
The only identified LLM application is McDonough, Nagar & Schoenfeld (RAST 2024), which used an LLM to generate topics from hand-collected activist disclosure texts and found board-seat-demand disclosures earn the highest returns; no 2022-2026 paper applying LLMs specifically to 13D Item 4 or to activist letter tone with return outcomes was found.

### Cited Findings
- McDonough, Nagar & Schoenfeld (2024): "hand-collects disclosure texts and generates topics using a large language model"; board-change campaigns have the largest return effects — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-024-09836-6); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381919610_Voluntary_disclosures_by_activist_investors_the_role_of_activist_expectations) (search summary).

## Key Question 8: Practical data — how 13D/13G/13F are structured on EDGAR, filing latency, identifying filer type

### Takeaway
Each filing has an SEC header separating SUBJECT COMPANY (target CIK/SIC) from FILED BY (filer CIK) with GROUP MEMBERS; 13D/13G primary documents are XML since Dec 2024 with exhibits (letters, agreements) as separate EX-99 files; acceptance timestamps are to the second and the cut-off is 10 p.m. ET; data.sec.gov JSON APIs and daily indexes allow polling at up to 10 requests/second. Filer type can be inferred from the cover-page type code (IA/PN/HC vs CO/IN/BD/IC/EP), the 13G rule box (13d-1(b)/(c)/(d)), Item 3 source of funds, and curated activist lists (SharkWatch 50 / Insightia).

### Cited Findings
- Header fields in a 13D/A (.txt submission): ACCESSION NUMBER 0000902664-24-006808; CONFORMED SUBMISSION TYPE SC 13D/A; PUBLIC DOCUMENT COUNT 2; FILED AS OF DATE 20241202; SUBJECT COMPANY block (Franklin Street Properties Corp, CIK 0001031316, SIC 6798 REITs); FILED BY block (Converium Capital Inc., CIK 0002043653, state A8 = Canada); GROUP MEMBERS lines listing Aaron Stern, Bruce Schanzer, Erez Asset Management LLC, Erez REIT Opportunities LP — [SEC .txt submission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031316/000090266424006808/0000902664-24-006808.txt); index shows acceptance 2024-12-02 10:45:14 with primary doc (83 KB) and EX-99.2 cooperation agreement (86 KB) — [filing index](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031316/000090266424006808/0000902664-24-006808-index.htm).
- Cover page per reporting person: CUSIP, name, citizenship, sole/shared voting and dispositive power, aggregate amount, percent of class, and "Type of Reporting Person" codes (IA, PN, OO, IN, HC, CO observed) — [Franklin Street 13D/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031316/000090266424006808/p24-3453sc13da.htm).
- Schedule 13D items: Item 3 source and amount of funds (SEC staff use it to confirm open-market purchases), Item 4 purpose of transaction (plans/proposals), Item 5(c) all transactions in the past 60 days (date, price, quantity), Item 6 contracts/arrangements (now including all derivatives referencing the class), Item 7 exhibits — [SEC Release 33-11253](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf); [Collin-Dufresne & Fos on Item 5(c)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18452/w18452.pdf) (search summary).
- Programmatic access: full-text search at sec.gov/edgar/search; daily indexes at /Archives/edgar/daily-index/ (by company, form type, CIK) in html/xml/json; submissions API data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json; max 10 requests/second with a declared User-Agent (company name and email); data back to 1994/95 — [SEC: Accessing EDGAR Data](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data).
- XML filing path: Schedule 13D/13G are entered through EDGAR Online Forms with required fields, validated by EDGAR; submission types SCHEDULE 13D, SCHEDULE 13D/A (and 13G equivalents); exhibits attached as files; combined tender-offer + 13D/A submissions remain non-XML — [EDGAR Filer Manual Vol. II v72](https://www.sec.gov/files/edgar/filermanual/archive/efmvol2-v72.pdf); schema in [Tech Spec v2.1](https://www.sec.gov/file/schedule-13d-13g-tech-specs-21).
- Latency stats: initial 13Ds in 2022 had median 10 calendar days from trigger to filing (9 for Prominent Activists); ~29% of initial 13Ds were late under the old rule; 13G medians were 40 days (QII), 45 (exempt), 10 (passive) — [SEC Release 33-11253 Tables 1, 4, fn. 732](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- Filer-type taxonomy used by SEC staff: "Prominent Activists" = Insightia "Activist Top Ten" (any year 2014-2023) or FactSet SharkRepellent "SharkWatch 50" (2021); "Other Institutions" = partnerships, corporations, investment advisers, banks; "Other Individuals" — [SEC Release 33-11253 fn. 692](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf).
- 13F: 13F-HR / 13F-NT / amendments, 45-day lag, 13F-CTR confidential requests since Feb 2023, structured data sets (SUBMISSION, COVERPAGE, INFOTABLE) — [SEC 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13ffaq.htm); [SEC 13F data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets).
- Free/commercial trackers referenced in search results: [Fintel activist feed](https://fintel.io/activists); [Apify 13D scraper](https://apify.com/wiry_kingdom/sec-13d-activist-filings); [WhaleWisdom backtesting whitepaper](https://whalewisdom.com/help/backtesting_whitepaper); [13D Monitor / Insightia] not reachable.

### Inferences
- A production pipeline: poll data.sec.gov submissions or the daily index for form types SC 13D, SC 13D/A, SCHEDULE 13D, SCHEDULE 13D/A, SC 13G, SCHEDULE 13G and amendments; parse the header for subject CIK vs filer CIK; join filer CIK to a curated activist list and to 13F filer CIKs (hedge fund identification via 13F-HR plus Form ADV); extract Item 4 text and EX-99 exhibits for NLP; compute the trigger date from Item 5(c) trades; timestamp events at EDGAR acceptance time.
- The 2022 distribution (994 lead filers, only 22 "prominent activists" filing 60 initial 13Ds) means a strategy restricted to top activists has ~5 events per month in the US; broadening to all institutional 13Ds with trade tables (~170-200 campaign-type filings per year per SEC Table 3) is needed for diversification.

- Could not read the XML technical specification zip; the exact element names for Item 4 and the exhibit linkage are unverified.
- EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov) and the "latest filings" browse endpoint were blocked/empty in this sandbox, so no live counts of 2025 13D filings were obtained.
