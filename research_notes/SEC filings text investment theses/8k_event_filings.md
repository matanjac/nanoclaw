# Form 8-K Current Reports as a Source of Text-Based Investment Signals

Scope: US SEC Form 8-K filings (and exhibits), Form 12b-25 (NT 10-K / NT 10-Q), 2004–2026 literature. Access note: the research sandbox blocked full-text retrieval from SSRN, ScienceDirect, Springer, AAA, NYU, Berkeley, arXiv and most university hosts; only sec.gov documents could be read in full. Effect sizes below are therefore taken from the papers' abstracts and from secondary summaries returned by search, and are labelled accordingly. Where a number could not be verified against the paper itself, it is marked "(abstract/summary-level)".

## Key Question 0 — Regulatory mechanics every 8-K thesis must respect (deadline, "furnished vs filed", Reg FD, EDGAR cutoff)

### Takeaway
Most 8-K items are due within four business days of the event, Items 2.02 and 7.01 are "furnished" rather than "filed", Item 2.06 impairments have a safe harbor that lets most impairments skip the 8-K entirely, and EDGAR's 5:30 p.m. ET cutoff means an after-hours filing carries the next business day's filing date. These mechanics determine whether a text signal is tradeable at the event, at the filing, or only in a later drift window.

### Cited Findings
- General Instruction B.1: "Unless otherwise specified, a report is to be filed or furnished within four business days after occurrence of the event. If the event occurs on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday ... the four business day period shall begin to run on, and include, the first business day thereafter." Item 1.05 (cybersecurity) and Item 5.08 run from the date the registrant "determines" the triggering fact. — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions (sec.gov PDF)](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- A registrant furnishing under Item 7.01 (Reg FD) or filing under Item 8.01 solely to satisfy Reg FD "must furnish such report or make such filing ... in accordance with the requirements of Rule 100(a) of Regulation FD, including the deadline" (i.e., simultaneously with intentional disclosure; promptly after unintentional disclosure). — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- General Instruction B.2: information furnished under Item 2.02 or Item 7.01 "shall not be deemed to be 'filed' for purposes of Section 18 of the Exchange Act", and all exhibits relating to Items 2.02/7.01 are deemed furnished unless the registrant specifies otherwise under Item 9.01. — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Item 2.02(a): when a registrant "makes any public announcement or release ... disclosing material non-public information regarding the registrant's results of operations or financial condition for a completed quarterly or annual fiscal period, the registrant shall disclose the date of the announcement ... and include the text of that announcement or release as an exhibit." Instruction 2 applies Reg S-K Item 10(e)(1)(i) (non-GAAP reconciliation rules) to Item 2.02 disclosures. — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Item 2.06 Instruction: "No filing is required under this Item 2.06 if the conclusion is made in connection with the preparation, review or audit of financial statements required to be included in the next periodic report ... the periodic report is filed on a timely basis and such conclusion is disclosed in the report." — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Item 5.02(a): when a director resigns or refuses to stand for re-election "because of a disagreement with the registrant ... on any matter relating to the registrant's operations, policies or practices", the registrant must describe the disagreement, file any written correspondence from the director as an exhibit, give the director a copy of the disclosure, and file the director's response letter by amendment within two business days. Item 5.02(b) only requires disclosure of "the fact that the event has occurred and the date of the event" for departures of the PEO, president, PFO, PAO, POO or any NEO. — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Item 5.02(c) Instruction: a registrant "may delay filing the Form 8-K containing the disclosures required by this Item 5.02(c) until the day on which the registrant otherwise makes public announcement of the appointment." — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Item 4.01 requires the Reg S-K Item 304(a)(1) disclosures (reasons, disagreements, "reportable events") and a separate 8-K for engagement of the new auditor; Item 4.02(a) requires the date of the non-reliance conclusion, the periods affected, "a brief description of the facts underlying the conclusion to the extent known", and whether the audit committee discussed it with the auditor; Item 4.02(b)/(c) covers auditor-initiated non-reliance and requires the auditor's agreement letter to be filed by amendment. — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- The SEC's Investor Bulletin lists the auditor-change red flags that must be disclosed (adverse/qualified opinion, disagreements, auditor advised that internal controls are inadequate, auditor unwilling to rely on management representations, auditor needed to expand audit scope, auditor found information affecting reliability of prior statements) and describes Item 2.02 as the mechanism by which "the press release [is included] as an exhibit." — [SEC Investor Bulletin: How to Read an 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/readan8k.pdf)
- EDGAR accepts filings 6:00 a.m.–10:00 p.m. ET on business days; a transmission begun after 5:30 p.m. ET is deemed filed on the next business day even though it is publicly available on EDGAR immediately. Example cited: "An 8-K accepted at 8:15 pm on Friday, August 28 is dated Monday, August 31." Section 16 forms (3/4/5) are exempt from the 5:30 cutoff up to 10:00 p.m. — [Latham & Watkins, "How does EDGAR's 5:30 pm filing cutoff work?"](https://wow.lw.com/Article/Index/186); [Colonial Filings](https://www.colonialfilings.com/blog/edgar-filing-cutoff-time-10-k/); [EdgarTools, "SEC EDGAR Current Filings Feed: The After-Hours Gap"](https://www.edgartools.io/edgar-live-feed-after-hours-filings/)
- Practitioner data vendors expose item-level 8-K search (e.g., Item 2.02, 4.01, 4.02, 5.02 endpoints and an "Earnings Results – Form 8-K Item 2.02 (2004–Present)" dataset), which confirms item tags are machine-readable from the EDGAR header. — [sec-api.io Item 5.02 API](https://sec-api.io/docs/form-8k-data-item5-2-search-api); [sec-api.io Item 4.01 API](https://sec-api.io/docs/form-8k-data-item4-1-search-api); [sec-api.io Item 2.02 dataset](https://sec-api.io/datasets/earnings-results-form-8-k-item-2-02); [EdgarTools 8-K docs](https://edgartools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/eightk-filings/)
- "The overwhelming majority of Item 2.02 filings are earnings releases: a public company issues a press release announcing financial results, then furnishes that press release to the SEC as Exhibit 99 to Form 8-K." — [Apify SEC EDGAR filings scraper description](https://apify.com/pradio/sec-edgar-filings)

### Inferences
- For Items 2.02/7.01 the text (EX-99.1 press release) is public via the wire before or simultaneously with the 8-K, so the 8-K itself is not an information event; any "text" thesis on earnings releases is really a thesis on the press-release text and should be timed from the wire timestamp, not the EDGAR acceptance time.
- For Items 2.05, 2.06, 3.01, 4.01, 4.02, 5.02 the 8-K is frequently the first public disclosure, and the event-to-filing lag (up to 4 business days, with firms frequently using the full window) creates both an insider "trading gap" (see Q5) and a filing-day event.
- The Item 2.06 safe harbor means the population of impairment 8-Ks is a self-selected minority (see Q4), so any 2.06 signal carries a selection-bias caveat.
- Because post-5:30 p.m. filings are dated the next business day, a researcher using EDGAR "filing date" will mis-align event windows by one day for after-hours filings; use the acceptance timestamp from the EDGAR index.

### Gaps
- The exact share of 8-Ks filed after 5:30 p.m. or on Fridays, and item-level breakdowns, could not be retrieved (Hudson Labs "Filing after hours: When it matters" and the EdgarTools after-hours post were blocked).
- I could not access the EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov) from the sandbox to document the exact query syntax for item filters.

## Key Question 1 — Item 2.02 and the earnings press release exhibit (EX-99.1): tone, guidance, non-GAAP, bloat, and conditional PEAD

### Takeaway
The best-documented text signal in the Item 2.02 exhibit is managerial tone: raw optimism predicts future performance and a same-window market reaction (Davis-Piger-Sedor; Henry), but "abnormal" tone (tone not explained by fundamentals) predicts a reversal of roughly 2.4% over one quarter and 4.7% over two quarters (Huang-Teoh-Zhang 2014, abstract-level). LLM summarisation ("bloat") work shows that stripping uninformative text makes sentiment more informative about the announcement return, but the strongest published drift evidence remains the abnormal-tone reversal.

### Cited Findings
- Davis, Piger & Sedor, "Beyond the Numbers: Measuring the Information Content of Earnings Press Release Language", Contemporary Accounting Research 29(3), 2012, pp. 845–868 (earlier version: St. Louis Fed WP 2006-005). Sample of approximately 24,000 earnings press releases 1998–2003, scored with DICTION. Findings: optimistic and pessimistic language "reliably predict future firm performance", there is "a positive association between optimistic language usage and future firm performance and a significant incremental market response to optimistic and pessimistic language usage." — [CAR 2012 (Wiley)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1911-3846.2011.01130.x); [St. Louis Fed WP 2006-005](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-005.pdf); [IDEAS summary](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedlwp/2006-005.html)
- Henry, "Are Investors Influenced By How Earnings Press Releases Are Written?", Journal of Business Communication 45(4), 2008, pp. 363–407. Rhetorical analysis plus capital-markets tests; "results suggest that tone influences investors' reactions" and an optimistic tone drives positive short-term reactions; also documents strategic non-GAAP presentation when GAAP earnings failed to increase. — [SAGE listing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0021943608319388); [PDF mirror](https://study.sagepub.com/sites/default/files/1%20Henry%202008_0.pdf)
- Huang, Teoh & Zhang, "Tone Management", The Accounting Review 89(3), 2014, pp. 1083–1113. ABTONE = residual positive tone after controlling for earnings, risk, complexity and other fundamentals. "Abnormal positive tone at the time of earnings press releases is a negative predictor of abnormal returns", with a more positive immediate reaction and "a more negative market response in one and two quarters subsequent." Summary-level magnitudes: "abnormal returns from tone management is 2.44% for one quarter (9.76% annualized) and 4.70% for two quarters (9.4% annualized)." ABTONE predicts negative future earnings/cash flows 1–3 years ahead, higher incidence of just meeting/beating thresholds, future restatements, SEOs and M&A, lower incidence of option grants. — [SSRN 1960376](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1960376); [UCI PDF](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/e/360/files/2012/08/Tone-Management.pdf); [AAA Commons summary](http://commons.aaahq.org/posts/b4ad070ca9); [paper note](https://fengzhi22.github.io/blog/2020/04/21/paper-note-Huang-et-al-2014)
- Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev, "Bloated Disclosures: Can ChatGPT Help Investors Process Information?" (June 2023, arXiv 2306.10224; BFI WP 2023-59). GPT summaries are shorter and more informative; disclosures are "bloated by about 70 percent"; regressing short-window abnormal returns around disclosure days on sentiment, "summary-based sentiment is more informative in explaining stock market reactions"; bloated disclosures "are associated with adverse capital market outcomes." (Note: the paper's corpus is MD&A sections and earnings conference-call transcripts, not the Item 2.02 exhibit itself; summary-level.) — [arXiv 2306.10224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10224v1); [BFI summary](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/insight/research-summary/bloated-disclosures-can-chatgpt-help-investors-process-information/); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/equation-how-chatgpt-revealed-bloat-corporate-disclosures)
- Feldman, Govindaraj, Livnat & Segal, "Management's Tone Change, Post Earnings Announcement Drift and Accruals", Review of Accounting Studies 15(4), 2010, pp. 915–953: a tone that becomes more negative than the prior year is associated with lower contemporaneous returns; tone change in MD&A is incrementally associated with drift beyond earnings surprise and accruals. (This is MD&A in 10-K/10-Q, not the 8-K exhibit, but is the canonical "tone change predicts PEAD" paper.) — [Bar-Ilan listing](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/managements-tone-change-post-earnings-announcement-drift-and-accr/); [SCIRP reference](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=3327807)
- "Textual fundamentals in earnings press releases" (Advances in Accounting, 2022) and "Managerial Ability, Tone of Earnings Announcements, and Market Reaction" are later extensions of the DPS/HTZ line. — [ScienceDirect listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0882611022000104); [CSU thesis](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/9s161672g)
- Non-GAAP: Bradshaw & Sloan (2002) documented the pre-Reg G rise of manager-disclosed non-GAAP metrics in press releases; a 2018 HBS/SSRN paper reports most S&P 500 firms announce non-GAAP earnings "on average 23% larger than GAAP earnings" and that high non-GAAP earnings predict abnormally high CEO pay; a 2021 Accounting Review review notes that "research finds little evidence of mispricing induced by non-GAAP reporting." — [Guest, Kothari & Pozen, "High Non-GAAP Earnings Predict Abnormally High CEO Pay"](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Shared%20Documents/conferences/2018-imo/GKP%20Non-GAAP%20Compensation%20Paper%20May%202018.pdf); [TAR 96(3) 2021](https://aaahq.org/portals/0/newsroom/2021/accr-96-03-1-25.pdf?ver=2021-06-04-163501-083); [Bradshaw & Sloan 2002](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227694936_GAAP_versus_The_Street_An_Empirical_Assessment_of_Two_Alternative_Definitions_of_Earnings)
- Information order in press releases also matters: Roulstone et al. study whether investors are influenced by the order of information in earnings press releases. — [Bauer/UH working paper](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/accy/research/documents/Roulstone-Are-Investors-Influenced-by-the-Order-of-Information-in-Earnings-Press-Releases.pdf)
- Machine-learning sentiment measures "offer significant improvement in explanatory power over dictionary-based measures" and explain filing-date returns where Loughran–McDonald lists only work in their original sample period. — [Frankel, Jennings & Lee, "Disclosure Sentiment: Machine Learning vs. Dictionary Methods", Management Science 2022](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4156)
- Market timing of earnings releases: deHaan, Shevlin & Thornock, JAE 60(1), 2015, pp. 36–55: "managers report bad news after market hours, on busy days, and with less advance notice, with earnings receiving less attention in these settings"; bad news is also reported on Fridays but lower Friday attention was not found, and negative returns occur when the market is notified of an upcoming Friday announcement. — [SSRN 2545966](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2545966); [IDEAS/JAE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v60y2015i1p36-55.html)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T1 (drift, slow-fund-capturable): compute abnormal tone of the EX-99.1 press release (residual of positive-minus-negative word share on earnings surprise, size, BM, volatility, accruals, loss dummy, etc.); short the top ABTONE decile / long the bottom after the announcement reaction, hold 1–2 quarters. Expected gross spread per HTZ is on the order of 2–5% over one to two quarters, but these are 1997–2007-era estimates measured before the method was widely published.
- Candidate thesis T2 (event-day): optimistic language predicts same-window 3-day CAR (DPS 2012) — this is an immediate reaction that is gone by the close of the filing day for firms that release during market hours; it only helps a fund that trades the press release at the wire timestamp, not at the 8-K.
- Candidate thesis T3: LLM-summarised sentiment of the earnings release (à la Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev) should dominate raw dictionary sentiment for predicting the announcement reaction; whether it predicts drift is untested in the papers found.
- Timing pitfall: after-hours earnings 8-Ks are furnished with a next-day filing date; bad-news releases cluster after hours and on busy days, so tone-based sorts partly load on the known "after-hours bad news" effect and should control for release time.

### Gaps
- Exact regression coefficients, t-statistics and holding-period alphas for DPS 2012 and HTZ 2014 could not be verified against the papers (full texts blocked); the 2.44%/4.70% HTZ figures come from a secondary summary.
- I found no 2022–2026 paper that applies an LLM specifically to the Item 2.02 EX-99.1 exhibit and reports post-announcement drift; the LLM work located (Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev; Lopez-Lira & Tang on headlines) uses other corpora.
- Guidance language (e.g., raise/lower/withdraw wording) within EX-99.1 was not covered by any paper located in this session.
- Evidence on non-GAAP "emphasis" (placement/prominence in the release) predicting returns post-Reg G was not located beyond the general "little evidence of mispricing" statement.

## Key Question 2 — Item 5.02: executive and director departures

### Takeaway
Item 5.02(b) only requires the fact and date of a departure, so the stated reason ("to pursue other opportunities", "personal reasons", "no disagreements") is voluntary text; the one recent paper found (Advances in Accounting, 2025) shows investors react negatively to CFO turnovers that precede restatements but not to CEO turnovers, and the classic Hennes-Leone-Miller (2008) result ties CEO/CFO turnover to irregularity (not error) restatements. No paper located quantifies returns conditional on the stated departure reason text.

### Cited Findings
- Item 5.02(b) mandates only "the fact that the event has occurred and the date of the event" for officer departures; disagreement details and resignation letters are mandated only for director departures under 5.02(a). — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Audit Analytics' July 2008 CEO/CFO departures briefing: for CFO resignations the most common reasons are that employment was obtained elsewhere or "pursuing other interests"; in 2007, 45 cases cited personal/health reasons and 42 cited "pursue other interests." — [Audit Analytics briefing (PDF)](https://www.auditanalytics.com/doc/briefing-do-trends-20080709.pdf)
- "Pre-restatement executive turnovers and investor reactions" (Advances in Accounting, 2025): "Investors react negatively to pre-restatement CFO turnovers, but the reactions to CEO turnovers are insignificant"; restating firms are significantly more likely to experience CEO/CFO turnover before the restatement announcement; at the later restatement announcement, firms with pre-restatement CEO turnover show more negative abnormal returns. — [ScienceDirect listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0882611025000276)
- Hennes, Leone & Miller, "The Importance of Distinguishing Errors from Irregularities in Restatement Research: The Case of Restatements and CEO/CFO Turnover", The Accounting Review 83, 2008, pp. 1487–1519. — [Reference listing](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=2440019)
- "Auditor-provided nonpublic signals of misreporting and CFO dismissal", Review of Accounting Studies (2025) — links auditor private signals to CFO dismissals (abstract not retrievable). — [Springer listing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-025-09915-2)
- Cohen, Jackson & Mitts' 8-K trading-gap data (see Q5) include Item 5.02 among the items where insiders trade between event and filing. — [SSRN 2657877](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2657877)
- An arXiv 2026 paper, "Disclosed Human-Capital Disruption and Firm-Specific Risk", builds an "Item 5.02 audit of exit-document pairs" to classify forced vs voluntary executive exits. — [arXiv 2608.14859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14859)
- Practitioner heuristic: CNBC reports Cramer's rule that unexpected executive resignations for undisclosed personal reasons are "almost always" a sign something is wrong. — [CNBC 2018](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/06/07/cramers-investing-rule-for-when-ceos-unexpectedly-resign.html)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T4: CFO (not CEO) departures where (a) the 8-K is filed under 5.02(b) only, (b) no successor is named in the same filing (so the 5.02(c) delay option was not used), (c) the stated reason is vague ("personal reasons", "pursue other opportunities") and (d) the filing is standalone rather than attached to an earnings release, are the sub-population most likely to precede Item 4.02 non-reliance filings. This is a composite inference from the Form instructions, the Advances in Accounting 2025 finding, and HLM 2008; it has not been tested as stated in any paper found.
- Because 5.02(a) forces disclosure of director disagreements and letters as exhibits, director-resignation 8-Ks with a letter exhibit (EX-17) are a rare, high-signal subset worth screening.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures CARs or drift conditional on the text of the departure reason (resigned vs terminated vs "pursue other"), nor one that quantifies the restatement/fraud hazard rate following vague-reason CFO departures.
- Sample sizes, windows and magnitudes for the Advances in Accounting 2025 CFO-turnover paper could not be retrieved.
- "Sudden" or "abrupt" departure definitions (e.g., effective immediately, no transition period) were not found in any located paper.

## Key Question 3 — Item 4.01 auditor changes and Item 4.02 non-reliance

### Takeaway
Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-Ks are the most reliably negative item: 8,006 filings 2004–2023 show CARs of −2.6% to −5.4%, with revenue-recognition errors and uncertainty language roughly doubling or tripling the reaction (Schroeder 2025, abstract-level), and a vendor study shows −1.1% at day +1 extending to −2% by day +20. For Item 4.01, "reportable events" wording drives a −2.75% three-day and −5.53% seven-day CAR (Whisenant et al. 2003).

### Cited Findings
- Schroeder, "Effects of Non-Reliance Disclosures in Form 8-K Filings on Stock Prices" (SSRN, posted Jan 31, 2025): 8,006 Item 4.02 disclosures 2004–2023; "cumulative abnormal returns of −2.6% to −5.4%, with revenue recognition errors triggering 114% more severe reactions than net income errors"; "disclosures expressing uncertainty about the impact of identified errors face three times harsher market responses than those with clearly stated effects, while SEC-identified issues do not amplify negative price reactions." — [SSRN 5118253](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5118253)
- sec-api.io event study: "From 2007 to 2023 (excluding 2021), Item 4.02 disclosures are associated with an average cumulative abnormal return (CAR) of −1.1% one day post-disclosure, extending to −2% over a 20-day event window." — [sec-api.io, Stock Price Reactions to Item 4.02](https://sec-api.io/resources/stock-price-reactions-to-item-4-02-disclosures-in-sec-form-8-k-filings)
- A dissertation on managerial (non-)reporting of restatements finds abnormal returns "significantly negative over short and longer term time frames for firms with Form 8-K (high disclosure) negative large and small impact restatements." — [OhioLINK ETD](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=case1220044485&disposition=inline)
- Lerman & Livnat (2010) identify Non-Reliance (4.02) as one of four new items with significant negative returns around both event and filing dates. — [SSRN 1126816](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=1126816)
- Whisenant, Sankaraguruswamy & Raghunandan, "Market Reactions to Disclosure of Reportable Events", Auditing: A Journal of Practice & Theory 22(1), 2003, pp. 181–194: "−2.75 percent cumulative abnormal return over a three-day announcement period and −5.53 percent over a seven-day period surrounding the disclosure of reportable events"; robust to controls for resignations and disagreements. — [AAA abstract](https://publications.aaahq.org/ajpt/article-abstract/22/1/181/5535/Market-Reactions-to-Disclosure-of-Reportable)
- Holowczak, Louton & Saraoglu, "Testing market response to auditor change filings: A comparison of machine learning classifiers", Journal of Finance and Data Science (2019): vectorises Item 4.01 text to predict the sign of the CAR around the 8-K filing date; reports "some improvement over the naïve classification method." — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405918818300096)
- Cooley's practitioner guide lists the Item 304 disclosures that must be made (reasons, adverse/qualified opinions, disagreements, reportable events, going-concern) and notes the auditor must furnish a letter stating whether it agrees (filed as EX-16). — [Cooley GovernanceBeat](https://governancebeat.cooley.com/accountant-changes-and-disagreements-disclosure-15-things-to-know/)
- Practitioner note: "auditor changes accompanied by reportable events are one of the strongest predictors of subsequent restatements." — [FilingRadar, "How to Read Item 4.02 Filings"](https://filingradar.app/learn/restatements)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T5 (event + drift): short Item 4.02 filers at the open after filing, holding ~20 trading days; the vendor study implies roughly −0.9% of additional drift after day +1 (−1.1% → −2.0%), and Schroeder's text conditioning (revenue recognition; "cannot yet quantify"/uncertainty language) identifies the sub-sample with 2–3x the reaction. Because Item 4.02 is usually the first public disclosure, the filing day is an information event, unlike Item 2.02.
- Candidate thesis T6: Item 4.01 filings whose text contains any Item 304(a)(1)(v) "reportable event" or an EX-16 auditor letter that disagrees with the registrant's characterisation are the subset with −2.75%/−5.53% reactions; the seven-day window exceeding the three-day window suggests some post-filing continuation, but the paper is from 2003 and the magnitudes are summary-level.
- Day-of-week/time-of-day: both items are commonly filed after hours (Niessner shows bad-news 8-Ks cluster after close and on Fridays), so the "day 0" reaction appears on the next session.

### Gaps
- Schroeder (2025) windows, benchmark and t-statistics could not be verified; the CAR range may mix windows.
- No study located isolates going-concern wording within Item 4.01 and its return effect.
- Post-filing drift beyond 20 days for Item 4.02 was not quantified in any retrievable source.

## Key Question 4 — Other items: 1.01/1.02 agreements, 2.03 debt, 2.04 triggering events, 2.05 exit costs, 2.06 impairments, 3.01 delisting, 3.02 unregistered sales, 8.01

### Takeaway
Lerman & Livnat (2010) is the only systematic cross-item study: Items 2.05, 2.06, 3.01 and 4.02 produce significant negative returns at both event and filing dates, and 2.05 and 2.06 show further post-filing drift of −1.1% to −2.5% over up to 90 days (larger when no 10-K/10-Q intervenes). Item 3.01 deficiency notices are negative and worse for equity/multiple deficiencies (Guragai 2022). Item 2.06 is filed in under 10% of impairment quarters, and no return study specific to Items 1.01, 1.02, 2.03, 2.04, 3.02 or 8.01 text was found.

### Cited Findings
- Lerman & Livnat, "The New Form 8-K Disclosures", Review of Accounting Studies 15(4), 2010. New (2004-expansion) items are over half of 8-K filings in 2005–2006; all items show abnormal volume and return volatility around event and filing dates; "Four newly disclosed items generate significant negative returns around both the event date and the filing date: Costs Associated with Exit or Disposal Activities [2.05], Material Impairments [2.06], Notice of Delisting…Transfer of Listing [3.01], and Non-Reliance on Previously Issued Financial Statements [4.02]." "Some of the items exhibit significant return drift in the expected direction for up to 90 days after the SEC filings"; 2.06 and 2.05 "have significant negative drifts between −1.1% and −2.5%"; in a subsample with no periodic filing in the next 90 days the drift is larger. — [SSRN 1126816](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=1126816); [NYU PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf); [NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/jspui/handle/2451/27579)
- Guragai, "Market response to stock exchange listing deficiency notices: Evidence from Nasdaq", Advances in Accounting 59 (2022): 3,425 quantitative deficiency notices to 1,018 companies 2004–2015; investors react negatively to the 8-K (Item 3.01) filings; reactions are less negative for firms with more institutional ownership and analyst following; "equity and multiple deficiency notice filings elicit more negative reactions and are also more likely to result in actual delisting compared to bid price deficiency notice filings." — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0882611022000359); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/advacc/v59y2022ics0882611022000359.html)
- Sanseverino & Suh, "Are Current Disclosures Current? Evidence from Form 8-K Impairment Filings", Journal of Accounting, Auditing & Finance (online Dec 2024; print 2026): 2005–2020, "an 8-K Item 2.06 is filed in only 9.84% of firm-quarters with 10-K/Q impairments"; filing is more likely in quarters with option grants and higher insider purchases, less likely when raising capital. — [SSRN 5074799](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5074799); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0148558X241301465)
- Covenant violations (Item 2.04 territory): Stice's UNC dissertation "The Market Response to Implied Debt Covenant Violations" finds negative reactions to earnings implying covenant violation; related work finds "returns are significantly negative in the months leading up to and including the SEC filing date for firms that disclose debt covenant violations", with a market response "between −1.7% and −2.2%" in certain windows, and positive abnormal returns up to 24 months after violations; Nini, Smith & Sufi document real effects (CEO turnover, restructurings, lower capex/dividends) after violations. — [UNC CDR](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/hx11xf484); [AUT working paper on disclosure of covenant violations](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/29964/401474.pdf); [Nini, Smith & Sufi (Lehigh mirror)](https://www.lehigh.edu/~jms408/Nini_2018.pdf); [Saunders et al., "CovenantAI"](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_6B2_P261.pdf)
- "Consequences of Prescribed Disclosure Timeliness: Evidence from Acceleration of the Form 8-K Filing Deadline", The Accounting Review 97(7), 2022, p. 429, and "Increased mandated disclosure frequency and price formation: evidence from the 8-K expansion regulation" examine the 2004 rule change's effect on price formation (abstracts not retrievable). — [TAR 97(7)](https://publications.aaahq.org/accounting-review/article/97/7/429/373/Consequences-of-Prescribed-Disclosure-Timeliness); [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326492831_Increased_mandated_disclosure_frequency_and_price_formation_evidence_from_the_8-K_expansion_regulation)
- Practitioner reference tables of all 8-K triggers and deadlines. — [Latham & Watkins Desktop Reference 2024](https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Desktop-Reference-8-K-Filing-Events-2024-Latham-Watkins.pdf); [WilmerHale "Keeping Current with Form 8-K" Dec 2024](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/documents/20241217-keeping-current-with-form-8-k-a-practical-guide-2024-update.pdf); [Cooley 8-K triggers guide](https://www.cooley.com/~/media/cooley/pdf/pg-guide-handbook/8k-triggers-guide-june-2016.ashx?la=e)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T7 (drift): short Items 2.05/2.06 filers for 60–90 days after filing, especially when the next 10-Q/10-K is more than 90 days away; the Lerman-Livnat −1.1% to −2.5% drift is modest and 2005–2006-vintage, so a modern re-test is required.
- Candidate thesis T8: Item 3.01 filings citing equity (stockholders' equity / market value) deficiencies or multiple deficiencies, in low-analyst/low-institutional names, are the most negative subset and have the highest realised delisting rate; text parsing of the notice type (bid price vs equity vs multiple) is the key feature.
- The low (9.84%) voluntary filing rate for Item 2.06 implies that a 2.06 filing is itself a managerial-choice signal; Sanseverino-Suh's finding that filing is more likely around option grants and insider buying means some 2.06 filings may be timed to depress the price before grants, which would predict reversal rather than drift for that subset.
- Item 2.04 is rare and often co-filed with 1.01 amendments (waivers/forbearance); the covenant literature suggests the negative information is largely in price by the filing, with positive long-run returns afterward, so a contrarian long thesis is at least as plausible as a short.

### Gaps
- No paper located studies Items 1.01, 1.02, 2.03, 3.02 or 8.01 text as return predictors; the only coverage is the cross-item volume/volatility result in Lerman-Livnat.
- Lerman-Livnat's item-level frequency table, per-item CARs and t-statistics could not be read (PDF hosts blocked).
- No study was found specifically on Item 2.04 8-K filings (as opposed to covenant violations disclosed in 10-K/10-Q).

## Key Question 5 — Systematic timing studies: event-to-filing lag, strategic timing (Fridays, after hours), attention, and the 8-K trading gap

### Takeaway
Managers file bad-news 8-Ks disproportionately after the close and on Fridays; Niessner finds a roughly 50 bp underreaction to negative Friday disclosures that persists about three weeks (working-paper, summary-level), while institutional attention (Bloomberg) spikes on both event and filing dates and retail attention does not (Ben-Rephael, Da, Easton & Israelsen 2022). Insiders earn ~42 bp per trade inside the four-business-day gap, rising to 163 bp on open-market purchases (Cohen-Jackson-Mitts).

### Cited Findings
- Niessner, "Strategic Disclosure Timing and Insider Trading" (SSRN 2439040; working paper, OSU-hosted PDF). Uses 8-K filings 2004–2014 including firms with public filings but non-public equity; managers "are 6% more likely to disclose negative news after the market closes on a Friday relative to other days"; "a significant under-reaction in cumulative abnormal returns of approximately 50 basis points when managers disclose negative news on Friday. The mispricing persists for three weeks and is not traded away because of limits to arbitrage"; managers expect to gain an average of $12,271 from a Friday negative disclosure if they plan to sell; top managers are "more than twice as likely to strategically time disclosures if the return under-reaction benefits their insider sales." — [SSRN 2439040](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2439040); [OSU PDF](https://files.fisher.osu.edu/department-finance/public/strategic_disclosure_timing_and_insider_trading.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272242536_Strategic_Disclosure_Timing_and_Insider_Trading)
- deHaan, Shevlin & Thornock, "Market (In)Attention and the Strategic Scheduling and Timing of Earnings Announcements", JAE 60(1), 2015, pp. 36–55: bad news is reported after hours, on busy days and with less advance notice, and earnings receive less attention in those settings; no lower attention on Fridays but negative returns when a Friday announcement is scheduled. — [SSRN 2545966](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2545966)
- Ben-Rephael, Da, Easton & Israelsen, "Who Pays Attention to SEC Form 8-K?", The Accounting Review 97(5), 2022, pp. 59–88: significant abnormal Bloomberg (institutional) attention on both 8-K event and filing dates, traditional media attention higher on filing days, no clear retail pattern; "traditional media coverage on the filing day appears to attract the attention of retail investors and leads to further price changes in the direction of the pre-filing day price change, and institutional investors exploit this price pressure via opportunistic liquidity provision"; significant price discovery occurs on the event date and the days between event and filing. — [AAA](https://publications.aaahq.org/accounting-review/article/97/5/59/336/Who-Pays-Attention-to-SEC-Form-8-K); [Notre Dame PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/8k.pdf); [Lehigh PDF (2018 draft)](https://www.lehigh.edu/~jms408/Israelsen_2018.pdf)
- Cohen, Jackson & Mitts, "The 8-K Trading Gap" (SSRN 2657877, Columbia/Harvard, 2015): dataset of 15,419 Form 8-Ks with insider trades during the event-to-filing gap; "systematic abnormal returns of 42 basis points on average, per trade"; open-market purchases during the gap earn 163 bp; profits increase with the length of the gap; basis of the proposed 8-K Trading Gap Act (2019/2020). — [SSRN 2657877](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2657877); [Columbia PDF](https://millstein.law.columbia.edu/sites/millstein.law.columbia.edu/files/content/images/SSRN-id2657877.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/insider-trading-8-k-trading-gap/); [Harvard Law Forum on 8-K Trading Gap Act](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2020/02/07/8-k-trading-gap-act/)
- Further evidence on weekday/time-of-day timing of earnings news is in "Further evidence on the strategic timing of earnings news: Joint analysis of weekdays and times of day" (JAE 2016). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410116300052)
- Attention measured by EDGAR downloads of 10-K/10-Q/8-K is used as a proxy for stakeholder attention in "Attention to corporate disclosure and earnings management: Evidence from downloads of SEC filings". — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278425424000875)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T9 (drift, ~3 weeks): among negative-news 8-K items (2.05, 2.06, 3.01, 4.01 with reportable events, 4.02, 5.02 CFO departures, NT filings), those accepted by EDGAR after 5:30 p.m. ET on a Friday or before a holiday have an incremental ~50 bp underreaction that resolves over about 15 trading days (Niessner). This is small per event; it is a portfolio overlay rather than a standalone strategy.
- Candidate thesis T10 (follow the insiders inside the gap): Form 4 trades filed between an event date and the subsequent 8-K (reconstructable ex post from the 8-K's stated event date) identify filings where insiders had an informational edge; an outside investor cannot trade during the gap but can condition on the 8-K's disclosed event date versus insider trade dates to screen for the Cohen-Jackson-Mitts pattern.
- The Ben-Rephael et al. result (media-driven retail price pressure on the filing day, exploited by institutions providing liquidity) implies that filing-day moves in the direction of the pre-filing move tend to reverse — i.e., a short-horizon contrarian overlay on heavily covered 8-K filings.

### Gaps
- Niessner's paper appears to be an unpublished working paper; I could not confirm a journal publication or the exact sample sizes/t-statistics.
- Lerman-Livnat's measurement of the average event-to-filing lag by item could not be retrieved.
- No study was found that quantifies 8-K drift conditional on acceptance timestamp (hour of day) rather than day of week.

## Key Question 6 — Late filings: Form 12b-25 (NT 10-K / NT 10-Q) and the stated reason

### Takeaway
NT filings produce an immediate drop (five-day CAR around −2.8% to −5.5% for NT 10-Q depending on the stated reason) followed by continued downward drift over the following months; the drift is weaker when "accounting reasons" are cited, meaning the stated-reason text sorts the sample into a priced (accounting) and an underpriced (corporate events/other) group (Bartov & Konchitchki 2017, summary-level).

### Cited Findings
- Form 12b-25 must be filed no later than one business day after the due date, grants an automatic 15-day (10-K) / 5-day (10-Q) extension, and "requires management to provide a narrative explanation of the reason for the late filing." — [Unfolding Values, "Form 12b-25 (NT 10-K / NT 10-Q): How It Works"](https://www.unfoldingvalues.com/learn/form-nt-12b25/); [Securities Lawyer 101](https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2019/06/03/form-12b-25-extension/)
- The SEC has brought enforcement actions over inaccurate Form NT narratives, i.e., the stated reason is a representation with liability. — [Olshan securities blog](https://www.olshanlaw.com/Securities-Law-Blog/the-sec-reminds-public-companies-that-all); [SEC enforcement of Form NT disclosure (PDF)](https://www.publiccompanyadvisoryblog.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2023/09/SEC-Enforcement-of-Form-NT-Disclosure-Requirements.pdf)
- Bartov & Konchitchki, "SEC Filings, Regulatory Deadlines, and Capital Market Consequences", Accounting Horizons 31(4), 2017: 2,115 firms over nine years; prices drop "as soon as firms filed Form NT"; "abnormal returns for both NT 10-Q and NT 10-K filers continue to drift downward during the post-filing months. However, this drift is less pronounced when accounting reasons underlie the delay"; accounting problems are the most frequent reason; average delay is 41 days when accounting reasons are cited versus 13 days for corporate events; sharpest five-day drops are for NT 10-Qs: −5.48% (multiple issues), −2.87% (corporate events), −2.83% (accounting reasons). — [Accounting Horizons PDF (Berkeley Haas)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/yaniv/files/Papers_Publications/SEC-Filings-Regulatory-Deadlines-Capital-Market-Consequences_Bartov-Konchitchki_AH.pdf); [Accounting Today summary](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/investors-punish-companies-for-late-financial-statements-after-filing-form-nt-with-the-sec); [CFO.com summary](https://cfo.com/financial-reporting-2/2017/11/shares-drop-cos-file-late-study); [NYU Stern research highlight](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/late-financial-filings-come-at-a-cost)
- Earlier conference version: Bartov, DeFond & Konchitchki, "Capital Market Consequences of Filing Late 10-Qs and 10-Ks" (Bocconi 2012). — [Bocconi slides](https://accounting.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/BDK_BocconiPresentation_June201220120605171817.pdf)
- A Rochester dissertation, "Late Filings, Restatements, and Limited Attention" (Hunter Land), links late filings to subsequent restatements and attention. — [Rochester repository](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/fileDownloadForInstitutionalItem.action?itemId=35820&itemFileId=188436)
- Audit Analytics maintains an overview of non-timely filing counts and reasons. — [Audit Analytics blog](https://blog.auditanalytics.com/non-timely-filings-an-overview/)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T11 (multi-month drift): short NT filers for 1–3 months after the NT, overweighting those whose stated reason is a "corporate event"/other (less priced at filing) and those that subsequently miss the 15-day/5-day grace period; the Bartov-Konchitchki result implies the accounting-reason group is more fully priced at filing, so the drift is concentrated in the non-accounting group. Magnitudes of the drift itself were not retrievable.
- The 41-day average delay for accounting-reason NTs implies a high probability of a subsequent Item 4.02 and a second negative event; chaining NT → 4.02 → restatement is a natural sequential screen.

### Gaps
- Post-filing drift magnitudes (months 1–6) and t-statistics in Bartov & Konchitchki could not be read.
- No paper located applies NLP/LLM classification to the Form NT narrative text itself.

## Key Question 7 — 2022–2026 LLM/GPT-based work on 8-K text

### Takeaway
The most directly relevant 2026 work (Dolphin et al., "Grounded Event Extraction from SEC 8-K Filings with a Fine-Grained Taxonomy") tags 292,984 8-Ks (2022–2026) into 119 event types with quote-grounded validation and reports precision rising from 12% to 96% with its quality score; a public reproduction package tests economic significance via standardised abnormal returns. Earlier deep-learning work (Stanford 2019) claims 8-K-based short-term price-move prediction, and the broader LLM-return literature (Lopez-Lira & Tang; Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev) shows GPT-derived sentiment/summaries are informative, but no located paper reports a clean out-of-sample 8-K-text long-short alpha with drift windows.

### Cited Findings
- Dolphin, Dursun, Blankenship, Adams & Pike, "Grounded Event Extraction from SEC 8-K Filings with a Fine-Grained Taxonomy" (arXiv 2607.08346; SSRN 7087858; 2026): three-tier taxonomy of 119 event types; two-stage pipeline with schema validation and fuzzy n-gram quote validation, then a quality score from re-reading the cited quote; applied to 292,984 filings 2022–2026 yielding 601,088 grounded event tags; LLM-judge evaluation of 5,125 tags shows precision "rises monotonically with the quality score, from 12% to 96%"; keeping only top-score tags retains 34% of tags at 96% precision, score ≥4 retains 55% at 93%. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08346); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08346v1); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7087858.pdf?abstractid=7087858&mirid=1)
- An open-source reproduction "extracts taxonomy-validated, quote-grounded event tags via LLMs, then proves economic significance through variance-standardized abnormal returns (SAR), Kruskal-Wallis tests, and bootstrap variance decomposition over CRSP data." — [GitHub: chirindaopensource/grounded_event_extraction_from_SEC_8K_filings](https://github.com/chirindaopensource/grounded_event_extraction_from_SEC_8K_filings)
- Stanford CS224N (2019) project "Attention-based Stock Price Movement Prediction Using 8-K Filings" asserts 8-Ks "have short and long term impact on stock prices" and that deep learning can predict short-term moves after 8-K events (class project; not peer-reviewed). — [Stanford PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs224n/cs224n.1194/reports/custom/15751328.pdf)
- RPI's "Predicting Firm Financial Performance from SEC Filing Changes" compares 8-K content to year-over-year 10-K changes for performance prediction. — [RPI PDF](https://cs.rpi.edu/~zaki/PaperDir/CSEM23.pdf)
- Han, Li, Qiao & Zheng, "Structured Event Representation and Stock Return Predictability" (arXiv 2512.19484, Dec 2025; SSRN 5952915): LLM-extracted subject-action-object event triplets from news feed an attention model that beats other text-driven cross-sectional return forecasters out of sample (news corpus, not 8-Ks). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19484); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5952915)
- Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?": ChatGPT headline scores predict next-day returns and subsume other sentiment measures. — [UCLA Anderson PDF (2024 version)](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/document/2024-04/4.19.24%20Alejandro%20Lopez%20Lira%20ChatGPT_V3.pdf)
- Look-ahead bias warning for GPT-based return prediction. — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17322)
- Surveys: "The New Quant: A Survey of Large Language Models in Financial Prediction and Trading" (2025) and "A Review of Large Language Models for Stock Price Forecasting from a Hedge-Fund Perspective" (2026). — [arXiv 2510.05533](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05533); [arXiv 2605.05211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05211)
- Event-driven trading systems using LLMs ("Janus-Q", ACM ICAIF 2026) and an "entropy-based" LLM strategy report statistically significant abnormal returns on news, not specifically on 8-Ks. — [arXiv 2602.19919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19919)

### Inferences
- Candidate thesis T12: use a Dolphin-style fine-grained event taxonomy (119 types, quote-grounded) rather than the ~30 SEC item numbers; the economic test should be per-event-type SAR over [0,+1], [+2,+20] and [+21,+60] windows, with the quality score as a filter. The public repo gives a ready template.
- Because 8-K items are already machine-tagged, LLM value-add lies in (a) sub-typing within an item (e.g., 5.02 forced vs voluntary; 4.02 revenue-recognition vs other; 3.01 bid-price vs equity), and (b) exhibit-level extraction (EX-99.1 guidance changes, EX-10 agreement terms, EX-16/EX-17 letters).
- All GPT-based backtests before the model's training cutoff are exposed to look-ahead bias; treat pre-2023 "GPT predicts 8-K returns" results as in-sample unless the authors use point-in-time models.

### Gaps
- Dolphin et al.'s actual return-economic-significance numbers (SAR by event type) could not be read from the paper.
- No peer-reviewed 2022–2026 paper was found that reports a long-short alpha, Sharpe or t-statistic from LLM-classified 8-K text with explicit holding windows.

## Key Question 8 — Practical data notes (item tagging, latency, exhibits with rich text)

### Takeaway
8-K items are tagged in the EDGAR filing header and are queryable by item number via EDGAR full-text search and commercial APIs; the richest free text sits in exhibits (EX-99.1 press releases for 2.02/7.01/8.01, EX-10 agreements for 1.01, EX-16 auditor letters for 4.01, EX-17 director letters for 5.02(a)), and the four-business-day rule plus the 5:30 p.m. ET cutoff define the latency structure.

### Cited Findings
- Commercial endpoints exist for item-filtered 8-K retrieval (Item 2.02, 4.01, 4.02, 5.02), implying item tags are reliably parsed from the EDGAR header. — [sec-api.io Item 4.02 API](https://sec-api.io/api-reference/form-8k-item-4-02); [sec-api.io Item 4.01 API](https://sec-api.io/api-reference/form-8k-item-4-01); [sec-api.io Python example](https://sec-api.io/docs/form-8k-data-search-api/python-example)
- Item 2.02 requires the press release text as an exhibit; Item 5.02(a) requires director correspondence as an exhibit; Item 4.02(c) requires the auditor's letter by amendment; Item 1.01 Instruction 4–6 govern filing the agreement as an exhibit. — [SEC Form 8-K General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)
- Filing-date vs acceptance-time rule (5:30 p.m. ET). — [Latham & Watkins](https://wow.lw.com/Article/Index/186)
- Four-business-day deadline and insider trading inside that window. — [Cohen, Jackson & Mitts, SSRN 2657877](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2657877)
- A 2024 Latham "Desktop Reference: Form 8-K Filing Events" and WilmerHale's Dec 2024 guide list each item, its trigger and timing. — [Latham 2024](https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Desktop-Reference-8-K-Filing-Events-2024-Latham-Watkins.pdf); [WilmerHale 2024](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/documents/20241217-keeping-current-with-form-8-k-a-practical-guide-2024-update.pdf)

### Inferences
- A research pipeline should key on (filer CIK, acceptance datetime, item list, exhibit types) rather than filing date; align returns to the next trading session when acceptance is after 4:00 p.m. ET, and record whether a press release (EX-99.x) preceded the filing to separate "8-K as news" (2.05/2.06/3.01/4.0x/5.02) from "8-K as archive" (2.02/7.01).
- Items with the richest exhibit text for LLM extraction: 2.02 (EX-99.1 earnings release), 1.01/1.02 (EX-10 agreements), 4.01 (EX-16 letters), 5.02(a) (EX-17 letters), 8.01 (misc. press releases, often litigation/regulatory).

### Gaps
- Could not verify current EDGAR full-text search query syntax for item filtering (efts.sec.gov blocked in sandbox).
- No source quantifying the average event-to-filing lag by item for recent years was retrievable.
